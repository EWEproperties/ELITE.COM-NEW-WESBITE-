Location

Casares is located just 16 kilometres from Estepona and forms a typical postcard of an Andalusian town. It is one of the more beautiful white villages of Malaga on the Costa del Sol. The layout of the houses, very picturesque, has earned it the nickname of the hanging town.

Casares' origins date back to Iberian and Phoenician times, although its name comes from Roman domination; in fact, the name Casares is influenced by the name of Julio Cesar, who in 61 BC visited the famous Baños Hediondos de Carratraca to cure himself of a skin disease using sulphurous waters, making the place very famous throughout Rome for its healing properties.

The steep and winding streets of Casares are the perfect place to relax, discover corners that don't appear in guidebooks, local craft shops and small bars in the city to stop at along the way.

Casares Castle

Situated at the top of the town, it overlooks the valleys, hills and coastal plains stretching from Serranía de Ronda to the Bay of Algeciras, allowing a magnificent view of parts of the province of Málaga and Cadiz This is one of the most picturesque and beautiful corners of the municipality and a must-visit.

Hedionda Baths

They say that the devil gave his last breath here when he was banished by Santiago. This is one of the legends that the popular imagination perpetuates to justify the smell of sulphur in the waters. These known as the Baños de la Hedionda, a resource of cultural significance, are one of the historical landmarks of the Utrera massif and of Casares in general. It is all about the Iron Sulphur Baths, located in a beautiful and advantageous place that man has been able to use since Roman times.
