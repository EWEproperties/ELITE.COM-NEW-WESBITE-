Location

Dénia is a picturesque port town located on the Costa Blanca, just above Jávea. This seaside resort is located off the coast of the Balearic Islands (Ibiza, Majorca, Minorca), between Valencia and Alicante. To get there by car, take the A7 motorway from Valencia or Alicante. The journey by car from both cities takes about an hour. There are international airports in both Valencia and Alicante.

Among the many tourist resorts on the Costa Blanca, Dénia is one of the most attractive. It is not only the beautiful beaches for which the “White Coast” is famous, but also a beautiful historic centre, an extraordinary artistic heritage, an active yacht harbour and a spectacular natural environment.

Dénia is a very cosmopolitan city with an interesting mix of buildings reflecting its colourful history. Its main attraction is the 16th-century castle located above the city on a small hill. The castle has been thoroughly restored and houses a small archaeological museum. It also has pleasant gardens. The castle offers magnificent views of the sea and the town.

The landscape of Denia is dominated by the impressive silhouette of Montgó, an 800-metre-high mountain located a short distance from the coast. Together with the surrounding area, it forms the El Montgó Nature Reserve, where you can find 650 different species of plants and a rich fauna (eagles, peregrine falcons, owls, foxes, wild boars, genets). The protected area offers many trails that are worth exploring by bike or on foot.

The gastronomic sector draws inspiration from the Mediterranean diet and concepts such as Slow Food, promoting healthy food based on local resources and products to ensure more sustainable development. Dénia also emphasises the importance of a cross-cutting approach and the collaboration of multiple stakeholders in promoting gastronomy through the development of a wide range of activities and events involving both local and international players, such as the International Red Prawn Creative Cuisine Competition in Dénia, which attracts 50 chefs from around the world every year.

The city's large and impressive port, which serves all routes between Dénia and the Balearic Islands, deserves special attention. It offers ferry connections throughout the year, and in the summer the number of passengers using the service is exceptionally high. For many tourists who spend their holidays in this city, the idea of a day trip to the amazing beaches of Ibiza, Majorca or Formentera is simply perfect.

The beaches of Dénia are the main tourist attraction of the region for many. The city has almost 15 km of coastline, divided into two parts: Las Rotas is characterised by rocky and uncrowded coves, while Las Marinas has long, sandy and comfortable beaches.

Both offer many opportunities for fun: you can dive or snorkel, discovering the magnificent seabed of Las Rotas, as well as enjoy water sports in the Las Marinas area, including windsurfing, kitesurfing, stand-up paddleboarding and kayaking.
