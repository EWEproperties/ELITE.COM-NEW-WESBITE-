Location

Vera Playa is a lively seaside town offering a wide selection of restaurants, bars, and shops. Its wide, sandy beaches offer numerous opportunities for relaxation and water sports.

  • Almeria Airport: ±1 hour drive
  • Murcia Airport: ±1 hour and 20 minutes drive
  • Alicante Airport: ±2 hours and 20 minutes drive
  • A paradise with five kilometers of beaches

Those looking for a beach holiday to relax, sunbathe, and swim can enjoy various areas characterized by fine sand and crystal-clear waters. This year, Vera is also celebrating the renewal of the Q for quality of Las Marinas-Bolaga beach, a large area measuring 1,350 square meters and an average width of 200 meters, along which runs an attractive promenade; as well as the Blue Flag for El Playazo, a beach renowned worldwide for its extensive naturist area. These are joined by the beaches of Puerto Rey and Cala Marqués. All of them offer the opportunity to enjoy a drink at bars, beach bars, or exclusive beach clubs.

Nature, Leisure, and Outdoor Sports

Vera is also a place that offers the opportunity to enjoy nature in its surroundings, especially to explore the ecosystem of two wetlands: Salar de los Canos, next to El Playazo, which extends over 57 hectares and is a refuge and feeding lagoon, especially for waterbirds; and Puerto Rey Lagoon, next to the beach of the same name, where many birds migrate between Africa and Europe rest and nest.

In addition to enjoying nature, Vera offers numerous opportunities for various outdoor sports, from golf, where you can even play croquet, and water activities such as windsurfing, paddle surfing, kitesurfing, kayaking, catamaraning, and sailing, to rugby, beach volleyball, and tennis, among others.

Heritage, Culture, and Gastronomy

This Mediterranean municipality boasts over two millennia of history, and one of its heritage remnants is Cerro del Espíritu Santo (CES), the ancient Nasrid city of Barya, declared a cultural asset. Other landmarks worth visiting include the Plaza Mayor of Vera, home to the exceptional Church of Our Lady of the Encarnacion, a Mudejar-Andalusian structure; the Fuente de los Cuatro Caños, of Arabic origin; or Plaza de Toros, considered the oldest bullfighting arena in Almeria.

The municipality's history is also reflected in its gastronomy, through the aromas, colors, and flavors that reveal the Hispano-Arabic heritage of Al-Andalus, which still survives in spices, dishes, and the most avant-garde recipes, such as ajo colorado or gurullos with rabbit.
