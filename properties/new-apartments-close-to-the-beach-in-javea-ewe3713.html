Location

Located on the picturesque Costa Blanca, in the northern part of Alicante province, lies the charming seaside town of Jávea (also known as Xàbia). With its prime location on the Mediterranean coast and a wide bay protected on two sides by stone promontories, Jávea has become a popular destination for tourists, with a significant population of 35,000 foreigners who have made this town their home. In fact, Jávea is renowned for having the world's most favorable microclimate, second only to Rio de Janeiro.

Visitors can enjoy breathtaking views from the village of Jávea, which offers panoramic vistas of the coast of Ibiza, located approximately 90 kilometers away. A visit to the highest point in the area, Mount Montgo, which stands over 750 meters tall, is a must for tourists seeking a bird's-eye view of the city and the surrounding islands of Portichol and Ambolo.

Beach lovers can indulge in the sun and sea at popular beaches such as Arenal, Sardinella, Granadella, and Barraca. In addition to swimming, sunbathing, and diving in the crystal-clear waters, Jávea's city center offers visitors the chance to explore charming historic tenement houses, churches, atmospheric restaurants, and shops, as well as many service points where one can easily communicate in various foreign languages. Don't miss the picturesque seaside passage that offers a stunning panorama of the Mediterranean Sea.

The town's fishing port is also a must-see, offering fresh seafood such as shrimp, squid, and sardines caught daily between Jávea and Ibiza. The nearby sailing club is another popular spot, showcasing impressive yachts and motorboats owned by enthusiasts from all over Europe.

In the extensive Montanar district, visitors can enjoy sandy, pebbly, or rocky beaches, as well as famous chiringuitos beach bars offering exotic drinks throughout the season. The district is dotted with representative single-family houses in traditional style, covered with tiles and furnished with arcades, glass, and concrete. The area is separated by a road from less frequented rocky beaches.
