Location

Discover Jávea, a unique seaside town located on the Mediterranean coast in the northern part of the province of Alicante, on the Costa Blanca. Jávea, also known as Xàbia, is a hidden gem that attracts many tourists from all over the world. In fact, almost half of Jávea's 35,000 inhabitants are foreigners who have settled here.

One of the most important features of Jávea is its favourable microclimate. Scientists have determined that it has, after Rio de Janerio, the most favourable microclimate in the world. With more than 300 days of sunshine a year, it is the ideal place for sun seekers.

Jávea offers exceptional views of the surrounding area, including the Ibiza coastline some 90 kilometres away. Ferries run regularly from Jávea to the popular island. For those who prefer a more elevated view, Mount Montgo, at over 750 metres, is the highest point in the area and offers panoramic views of the town and the surrounding islands of Portichol and Ambolo.

Jávea is a paradise for lovers of relaxation by the sea. It boasts the popular beaches of Arenal, Sardinella, Granadella and Barraca. These beaches are ideal for sunbathing, swimming and snorkelling in the crystal clear sea. Arenal is the most famous beach in Jávea, with soft golden sand, perfect for a day spent lounging in the sun. Sardinella beach is smaller and more secluded, ideal for those looking for a quieter beach. Granadella beach is one of the most beautiful beaches in Jávea, located in a bay surrounded by cliffs covered with pine trees. Barraca beach is the calmest beach, ideal for those who want to enjoy the sea in a relaxing atmosphere.

The town centre of Jávea is full of history, charm and a magical atmosphere. There are historic townhouses and churches, as well as numerous atmospheric restaurants and shops. Visitors can easily communicate in foreign languages, as many service points cater for foreign tourists. The seafront walkway in the city centre offers a stunning panorama of the Mediterranean.

The fishing port of Jávea is well worth a visit, where visitors can sample fresh prawns, squid, sardines and other treasures caught daily between Jávea and Ibiza. At the nearby Yacht Club, visitors can admire the impressive yachts and motor boats from all over Europe, patiently waiting for their owners' summer holidays. Walking along the harbour promenade along the bay, visitors pass the Gorgos riverbed, which is dry for most of the year and forms a natural boundary between the harbour district and the Montanar district.

Montanar is the most extensive district of Jávea, where visitors can find the most important beaches in the bay, whether sandy, pebbly or rocky. The famous chiringuitos beach bars located on the beaches offer exotic drinks throughout the season. The development of this neighbourhood consists mainly of stately single-family houses in the traditional style, with tiled roofs and arcades and modern glass and concrete. Separated by road from the less frequented pebbly beaches, Montanar is a picturesque neighbourhood well worth exploring.
