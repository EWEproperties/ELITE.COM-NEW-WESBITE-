Location

Nestled along the sun-kissed shores of Costa Blanca, in the northern reaches of Alicante, you'll find the charming town of Jávea, affectionately known as Xàbia. With its picturesque bay cradled between rugged stone cliffs, it's no wonder this coastal haven draws tourists from all corners. In fact, nearly half of its vibrant 35,000-strong community hail from foreign lands, choosing to make Jávea their home. And here's a fun tidbit: scientists claim that, after Rio de Janeiro, Jávea has the world's second most delightful microclimate!

From Jávea's quaint streets, you're treated to breathtaking vistas stretching all the way to Ibiza's coast, a mere 90 kilometers away. And if you're itching for an island adventure, hop on one of the regular ferries from nearby Denia.

For those seeking a bird's-eye view, Mount Montgo, soaring over 750 meters, is the place to be. From its peak, you can gaze down at the town, the shimmering sea, and even the distant islands of Portichol and Ambolo. It's a sightseeing must-do!

Beach bums, rejoice! Sandy stretches like Arenal, Sardinella, Granadella, and Barraca beckon with their crystal-clear waters, perfect for a lazy sunbath or a refreshing snorkel.

Venture into Jávea's heart, and you'll be transported back in time. Historic buildings, atmospheric eateries, bustling shops, and a mosaic of languages fill the air. Don't miss the seaside promenade, where the vast expanse of the Mediterranean unfolds before you.

For a taste of the sea, the local fishing port is a treasure trove. Freshly caught shrimps, calamari, and sardines are just some of the day's haul between Jávea and Ibiza. And as you stroll by the nearby marina, sleek yachts and boats stand in silent anticipation of their next voyage. The Gorgos riverbed, often dry, serves as a natural divider between the bustling port and the serene Montanar district.

Speaking of Montanar, this sprawling district is the beach capital of Jávea. Whether you prefer sandy shores or rocky retreats, there's a spot for you. And as the sun sets, beach bars, known as chiringuitos, come alive, serving exotic concoctions. The district's architecture is a blend of tradition and modernity, with homes showcasing classic tiles and arches alongside contemporary glass and concrete designs, all overlooking the less-trodden rocky beaches.
