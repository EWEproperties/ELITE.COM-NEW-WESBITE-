Location

Discover Jávea, the unique seaside town located on the Mediterranean coast in the northern part of the province of Alicante, on the Costa Blanca. Jávea, also known as Xàbia, is a hidden gem that attracts many tourists from around the world. In fact, almost half of the 35,000 population of Jávea consists of foreigners who have made it their home.

One of the most significant features of Jávea is its favorable microclimate. Researchers have determined that it has the most favorable microclimate in the world after Rio de Janerio. With over 300 days of sunshine per year, it is an ideal destination for sun-seekers.

Visitors to Jávea are spoiled for choice when it comes to stunning views. The village of Jávea offers exceptional views of the surroundings, including the coast of Ibiza, which is about 90 kilometers away. Ferries run regularly from Jávea to the popular island of Ibiza. For those who prefer a more elevated view, Mount Montgo, over 750 meters high, is the highest point in the area and offers panoramic views of the city and the surrounding islands of Portichol and Ambolo.

Jávea is a paradise for lovers of relaxation, with its popular beaches of Arenal, Sardinella, Granadella, and Barraca. These beaches are perfect for sunbathing, swimming, and diving in the crystal-clear sea. Arenal is the most famous beach in Jávea, with its soft golden sand, perfect for a day spent lounging in the sun. Sardinella beach is smaller and more secluded, perfect for those looking for a quieter beach experience. Granadella beach is one of the most beautiful beaches in Jávea, nestled in a cove surrounded by cliffs covered with pine trees. Barraca beach is the most peaceful beach, ideal for those who want to enjoy the sea in a relaxed atmosphere.

The city center of Jávea is full of history, charm, and atmosphere. It is home to historic tenement houses and churches, as well as numerous atmospheric restaurants and shops. Visitors can easily communicate in foreign languages, as many service points cater to foreign tourists. The seaside passage in the city center offers a stunning panorama of the Mediterranean Sea.

Jávea's fishing port is worth a visit, where visitors can sample fresh shrimp, squid, sardines, and other treasures caught daily between Jávea and Ibiza. In the nearby sailing club, visitors can admire impressive yachts and motor boats from all over Europe patiently waiting for their owners' summer vacations. Walking along the port promenade along the bay, visitors will pass the Gorgos River bed, which is dry for most of the year, and is a natural border between the port district and the Montanar district.

Montanar is the most extensive district of Jávea, and it is where visitors can find the most important beaches in the bay, both sandy and pebbly or rocky. The famous chiringuitos beach bars located on the beaches offer exotic drinks throughout the season. The buildings in this district are mainly representative single-family houses in a traditional style, covered with tiles and arcades, modernly furnished with glass and concrete. Separated by a road from less-frequented rocky beaches, Montanar is a picturesque district that is worth exploring.
