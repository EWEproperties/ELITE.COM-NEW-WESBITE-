Location

Discover Jávea, a unique seaside town located on the Mediterranean coast in the northern part of the province of Alicante, on the Costa Blanca. Jávea, also known as Xàbia, is a hidden gem that attracts many tourists from all over the world. In fact, almost half of Jávea's 35,000 inhabitants are foreigners who have settled here.

One of the most important features of the city of Jávea is its favorable microclimate. Scientists have determined that it has the most favorable microclimate in the world after Rio de Janeiro. With over 300 sunny days a year, it is the perfect place for sun seekers.

Jávea offers exceptional views of the countryside, including the coast of Ibiza, approximately 90 kilometers away. Ferries run regularly from Jávea to the popular island. For those who prefer a more elevated view, Mount Montgo, at over 750 meters high, is the highest point in the area and offers panoramic views of the city and the surrounding islands of Portichol and Ambolo.

Jávea is a paradise for lovers of relaxation by the sea. It boasts the popular beaches of Arenal, Sardinella, Granadella and Barraca. These beaches are perfect for sunbathing, swimming and snorkeling in the crystal clear sea. Arenal is the most famous beach in Jávea, with soft golden sand, perfect for a day spent lounging in the sun. Sardinella Beach is smaller and more secluded, ideal for those looking for a quieter beach. Granadella beach is one of the most beautiful beaches in Jávea, located in a bay surrounded by cliffs covered with pine trees. Barraca Beach is the quietest beach, ideal for those who want to enjoy the sea in a relaxed atmosphere.

The city center of Jávea is full of history, charm and a magical atmosphere. There are historic tenement houses and churches, as well as numerous atmospheric restaurants and shops. Visitors can easily communicate in foreign languages, as many service points serve foreign tourists. From the seaside passage in the city center there is a stunning panorama of the Mediterranean Sea.

The fishing port of Jávea is well worth a visit, where visitors can taste fresh shrimps, calamari, sardines and other treasures caught daily between Jávea and Ibiza. At the nearby sailing club, visitors can admire impressive yachts and motor boats from all over Europe, patiently waiting for their owners' summer holidays. Strolling along the harbor promenade along the bay, visitors pass the Gorgos River bed, which is dry most of the year and forms a natural boundary between the harbor district and the Montanar district.

Montanar is the most extensive district of Jávea, where visitors can find the most important beaches in the bay, both sandy and rocky or rocky. The famous chiringuitos beach bars located on the beaches offer exotic drinks throughout the season. The buildings of this district are mainly representative single-family houses in a traditional style, covered with tiles and arcades, modernly equipped with glass and concrete. Separated by a road from less frequented pebbly beaches, Montanar is a picturesque neighborhood worth exploring.
