Location

Casares is a charming municipality in the province of Málaga, within the autonomous region of Andalusia in southern Spain. Situated on the border with the province of Cádiz, it forms part of the western Costa del Sol. Casares is considered one of the finest examples of a traditional Andalusian white village, famous for its narrow winding streets, steep alleys, and whitewashed houses. Located just 16 kilometres from Estepona, it offers visitors an authentic Andalusian atmosphere and breathtaking scenery.

According to local history, the name Casares is linked to Julius Caesar, who is believed to have visited the nearby sulphur baths in 61 BC to treat a skin condition. The healing properties of these mineral-rich waters became renowned throughout the Roman Empire.

Walking through the picturesque streets of Casares is a wonderful way to discover hidden corners, local artisan shops, traditional tapas bars, and the authentic charm of Andalusian life.

Casares Castle

Perched on the highest point of the village, Casares Castle overlooks valleys, rolling hills, and the Mediterranean coastline stretching from the Serranía de Ronda to the Bay of Algeciras. The castle offers breathtaking panoramic views across the provinces of Málaga and Cádiz and is one of the municipality's most iconic landmarks.

Hedionda Thermal Baths

Legend has it that this was the place where the devil took his last breath after being defeated by Saint James. This local legend explains the distinctive sulphur scent of the waters. The famous Baños de la Hedionda are a protected cultural heritage site and one of the most important historical landmarks of Casares. These ancient iron-sulphur thermal baths, located beside the Albarrán stream near the border with Manilva, have been used since Roman times for their therapeutic properties. The historic bathhouse, with its vaulted ceilings and unique architecture, remains one of the area's most fascinating attractions.
