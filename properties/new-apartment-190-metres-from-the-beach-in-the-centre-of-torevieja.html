Location

Torrevieja, a city about 45 km from Alicante, is famous for its unique, healthy microclimate. This is largely attributed to the large salt deposits and two extraordinary lagoons, Laguna de Torrevieja and La Laguna de la Mata, in the heart of the city. These lagoons, one pink and the other green, are not only a visual wonder, but also a habitat for various species of birds, including flamingos. The pink hue of the Laguna de Torrevieja, caused by its high salinity, crustaceans and algae, makes it a unique attraction.

The green-watered Laguna de la Mata is home to the park's interpretation centre and a variety of unique flora, including Aleppo pines and orchids. The vineyards in the area, which produce the local wine de la Mata, are also worth a visit.

Torrevieja's climate is ideal for beach lovers. From the 2.5-kilometre-long Blue Flag Playa de la Mata to the accessible and lively beaches of Playa de los Locos and Playa del Cura, there is a beach for everyone. In the town centre, natural pools amidst the rocky coastline offer a unique swimming experience, enriched by nearby statues such as the Man of the Sea and Lola the Beauty. Cala Ferris and Cala Piteras, with their great views, palm trees and snorkelling opportunities, are not to be missed.

Strolling along the Vista Alegre promenade, you will be immersed in the lively atmosphere of the city with its many bars, restaurants, cafes and shops. When it comes to entertainment, Torrevieja boasts the Aquopolis water park and a nearby amusement park, as well as summer open-air discos.

Sports enthusiasts will find a paradise in Torrevieja, with facilities for football, handball, tennis, rugby, swimming and more. The city, which once only had an observation tower (‘the old tower’ or ‘torre vieja’), has been transformed into an architectural marvel.

With over 300 days of sunshine a year, Torrevieja is more than just a holiday destination; it is an inviting place to live or even move to.
