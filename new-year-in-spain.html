New Year in Spain: How Spaniards Welcome the New Year

By Anita Orłowska · 23 December 2025 · updated 26 October 2026 · 3 min read

In short: On New Year's Eve (Nochevieja) Spaniards usually have a long family dinner, then at midnight eat 12 grapes, one on each chime of the clock, for 12 lucky months. The countdown from Puerta del Sol in Madrid is shown on TV across the country. Other customs include red underwear and a glass of cava. Parties and street celebrations begin after midnight and go on until morning. 1 January is a public holiday, quiet and spent with family. Many of the grapes eaten that night come from the Vinalopó valley near Alicante.

New Year's Eve in Spain has its own rhythm. The evening is calm and family-centred, the most important moment lasts just twelve seconds, and the party starts only once it is over.

Here is how Nochevieja works, so you can celebrate like a local.

The family dinner

Nochevieja starts at home or in a restaurant with a long dinner, often with seafood, Iberian ham, lamb or fish, and turrón and other Christmas sweets for dessert. Restaurants offer special set menus; book well ahead, especially on the coast.

The 12 grapes of luck

Just before midnight everyone gathers in front of the television or in the town square. When the clock strikes twelve, you eat one grape on each chime: 12 grapes for 12 lucky months. It is harder than it sounds, and the laughter is part of the fun. Supermarkets sell peeled, seedless grapes in small cans for the occasion. The custom became popular in the early 20th century, and today many of the grapes come from the Vinalopó valley near Alicante, where bunches are grown in paper bags to ripen late.

Puerta del Sol and the party after midnight

The most famous countdown is at the clock of the Puerta del Sol in Madrid, broadcast live on TV. Many towns hold their own celebration in the main square, with music and sometimes fireworks. Only after the grapes and a toast with cava do people head out: parties, clubs and bars stay open until dawn, and some finish with hot chocolate and churros in the morning.

Other customs for luck

  • Wearing red underwear, ideally received as a gift.
  • Dropping a gold ring into your glass of cava for the toast.
  • Stepping into the new year with your right foot first.
  • In some coastal towns, a midnight or New Year's Day swim in the sea.

1 January

New Year's Day is a national public holiday. Most shops are closed, the streets are quiet in the morning, and families meet again for a long lunch. The festive season is not over yet: the big day for children is still to come on 6 January. Read Three Kings Day in Spain and Christmas in Spain.

Frequently asked questions

Why do Spaniards eat 12 grapes on New Year's Eve?

For luck: one grape on each of the 12 chimes at midnight, one for each month of the new year.

Where is the most famous New Year's Eve celebration in Spain?

At the Puerta del Sol in Madrid, where the clock chimes are broadcast live across the country.

Is 1 January a public holiday in Spain?

Yes. It is a national holiday and most shops and offices are closed.

What do Spaniards drink at New Year?

Usually cava, the Spanish sparkling wine, for the toast after the grapes.
