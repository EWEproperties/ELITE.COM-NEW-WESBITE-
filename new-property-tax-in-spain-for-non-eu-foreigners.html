Spain's Proposed 100% Tax for Non-EU Buyers: Where It Stands

By Anita Orłowska · 21 April 2025 · updated 24 October 2026 · 4 min read

In short: In January 2025 the Spanish government announced a tax of up to 100% of the price on homes bought by non-residents from outside the EU, and in May 2025 the governing Socialist group put a bill to Congress. As of October 2026 it has not been debated or passed, so it does not apply. It has no clear majority, and lawyers doubt it would survive EU and constitutional challenges. It would never affect buyers living in the EU or anyone tax resident in Spain. What did change is the golden visa: since 3 April 2025 buying property no longer gives a residence permit. Non-EU buyers today pay the same purchase taxes as everyone else.

Few announcements have caused as many questions from buyers as this one. The headline "100% tax on foreigners" spread around the world, but the details, and its current status, are much less dramatic. Here is what was proposed, where it stands and what it means if you are buying now.

What was proposed

In January 2025 Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez presented a package of housing measures. Besides more public housing and stricter rules for tourist flats, it included a tax of "up to 100%" of the value of homes bought by non-EU non-residents.

In May 2025 the Socialist parliamentary group registered a bill in Congress creating a new state tax on purchases of property by non-residents from countries outside the EU. As drafted:

  • the taxable base would be the highest of the declared price, the market value and the cadastral reference value,
  • the rate could reach 100%, so the tax could roughly double the cost of a home,
  • it would come on top of the normal purchase taxes,
  • it would not apply in the Basque Country and Navarre, which have their own tax systems.

Where it stands in October 2026

It is not law. The bill was registered but, as far as published records show, it has not been debated or voted on in Congress. The government has no secure majority for it: some partners oppose it as damaging to investment, others argue it does not go far enough.

There are also serious legal doubts. A rate of 100% could be judged confiscatory under the Spanish Constitution, and EU rules on the free movement of capital also protect investors from outside the EU. Tax advisers widely expect a challenge if it ever passes.

We will update this article if the bill moves. Until then, buyers from outside the EU pay exactly the same purchase taxes as Spanish and EU buyers.

Residence for tax purposes generally means spending more than 183 days a year in Spain or having your main business or family interests here. Our guide to resident or non-resident explains the rules.

What did change: the end of the golden visa

The change that is already in force is different. Since 3 April 2025, buying property worth €500,000 or more no longer gives a non-EU buyer a residence permit (the "golden visa"). Permits granted before that date can still be renewed. Non-EU buyers who want to live in Spain now need another route, such as the non-lucrative visa for people with their own income, the digital nomad visa or a work permit. For stays, see staying more than 90 days.

The main difference from EU owners is on rental income: EU residents pay 19% on the net rent after expenses. More in our guide to property and wealth taxes.

What this means if you are buying now

  • The proposed tax does not apply today. A purchase completed under current law is taxed under current law.
  • If you are from outside the EU and planning to buy, it makes sense not to delay unnecessarily, and to ask your lawyer to check the bill's status just before you sign.
  • If you plan to move to Spain, becoming tax resident would take you outside the scope of the proposal, but residence brings its own tax obligations. Take advice first.
  • Buyers living in the EU are not affected in any scenario.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a 100% tax on foreign buyers in Spain?

No. A bill for a tax of up to 100% on homes bought by non-EU non-residents was registered in Congress in May 2025, but as of October 2026 it has not been debated or passed.

Would the tax apply to buyers living in the EU?

No. It would only apply to non-residents from countries outside the EU. People living in the EU and anyone tax resident in Spain would not pay it.

Would British or Swiss buyers be affected?

If the bill passed as drafted, buyers living in the UK or Switzerland who are not tax resident in Spain would be affected, as neither country is in the EU.

Does buying property in Spain still give residency?

No. The golden visa for property investment ended on 3 April 2025. Existing permits can still be renewed.

What taxes does a non-EU buyer pay today?

The same purchase taxes as everyone else: transfer tax on resale homes, or VAT and stamp duty on new builds. Afterwards, rental income is taxed at 24% of the gross rent.
