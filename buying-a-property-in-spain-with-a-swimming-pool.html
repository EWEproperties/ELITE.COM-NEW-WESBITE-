Buying a Property with a Swimming Pool in Spain

By Aleksandra Kobus · 21 July 2025 · updated 24 October 2026 · 5 min read

In short: On the Costa Blanca and Costa del Sol a pool can be used for six months or more a year, and it is one of the features buyers and holiday guests ask for most. You can choose a private pool, which you control and pay for yourself, or a communal pool, whose costs are shared through the community fees. A private pool typically costs somewhere around €1,000 to €3,000 a year to run, depending on size, heating and whether you use a pool service. A built pool needs a municipal licence and must be registered with the Catastro, which raises the cadastral value and the IBI. Before you buy, check that the pool is legal and registered, and test the pump, filter and any heating.

A pool changes how you use a home in Spain. It also changes what the home costs to run, what you should check before you buy, and how easily you can rent it out. Here is what we tell our own buyers.

Is a pool worth paying for?

For most buyers on the coast, yes. In a holiday home a pool is often the first filter guests use on booking sites, and properties with a pool usually let for more weeks and at higher prices. When you sell, a pool widens your pool of buyers, particularly for villas.

It is not always the right choice. A private pool means running costs and work all year, even in the months you are not there. If you will only spend a few weeks a year in Spain and do not plan to rent out, an apartment with a well-run communal pool can give you most of the pleasure for a fraction of the effort.

Private or communal pool

  • Private pool: usual with villas and some townhouses and ground-floor apartments. Full privacy and control, but you pay all the costs and arrange the upkeep.
  • Communal pool: usual in apartment complexes and gated urbanisations. Maintenance, cleaning, insurance and, where required, the lifeguard are paid from the community fees, so the cost per owner is low. The community sets the opening season, hours and rules.

When you compare communal pools, look at the community fee, what it includes and how the pool is looked after. Our guide to the community of owners explains how fees and rules work.

What a private pool costs to run

These are typical ranges for an average villa pool (around 8 × 4 m) on the coast. Your figures depend on size, equipment, how long the season is and local prices.

A heat pump or other heating adds noticeably to the electricity bill but can stretch the season into spring and autumn. A salt system uses fewer chemicals, but its cell must be replaced every few years. Pools also add to the cadastral value of the property, so the yearly IBI is a little higher.

Licences, the Catastro and health rules

Licence. Building a pool needs permission from the town hall, either a building licence or a prior declaration, depending on the municipality. A pool built without it can be a planning breach, and legalising it later means a technical project and fees.

Catastro. A built pool must be declared to the Catastro, which updates the cadastral value and therefore the IBI. The Catastro uses aerial and satellite images to find undeclared pools and then regularises them, with a fee and back tax. Check the cadastral record (consulta descriptiva y gráfica) of the home you are buying: if the pool is missing, ask the seller to sort it out before completion.

Health rules. Royal Decree 742/2013 sets the water quality and safety criteria for pools used by groups of people, including community pools. The regions add their own rules, for example on lifeguards, signage and inspections. In the Valencian Community and Andalusia these mainly concern communal pools, and the community, not each owner, is responsible. For a single-family private pool the national decree applies only in part, but you are still liable if someone is hurt, so keep it clean and safe and have liability cover.

Water restrictions. In drought periods some towns limit filling or refilling private pools. Ask locally, and consider a cover to cut evaporation.

Holiday rentals. If you rent out, the pool must be safe for guests, and your insurance should cover liability towards them. Rules on tourist rentals themselves are in our guide to short-term rental rules.

What to check when viewing a property with a pool

  • Legal status: licence or approval for the pool, and that it appears in the Catastro and in the property's description.
  • Sun: how many hours the pool gets in spring and autumn, not only at midday in summer.
  • Condition: cracks, loose tiles, leaks (ask about water bills), the age of the pump, filter and any salt cell or heat pump.
  • Water system: chlorine or salt, automatic dosing, lighting.
  • Safety: depth, steps or a shallow area, a fence or cover if children will use it.
  • Communal pools: season and hours, rules for guests and tenants, and the community's maintenance costs in the last accounts.

If you will not be there all year, a pool service and someone to look after the property are worth arranging from the start.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to keep a private pool in Spain?

For an average villa pool, roughly €1,000 to €3,000 a year in total, covering chemicals, electricity, water, a pool service and small repairs. Heating raises the cost.

Does a swimming pool increase IBI?

Yes. A built pool is part of the cadastral value of the property, and the IBI is calculated on that value, so the yearly bill goes up somewhat.

What if the pool is not registered in the Catastro?

The owner must declare it. The Catastro also finds undeclared pools from aerial images and regularises them with a fee and back tax. When buying, ask the seller to regularise it before completion.

Does a community pool need a lifeguard?

It depends on the regional rules and the size and use of the pool. Where a lifeguard is required, the community arranges and pays for it through the fees.

Is a pool needed to rent out a holiday home?

It is not required, but it strongly affects demand and price on the coast, especially for villas and family stays.
