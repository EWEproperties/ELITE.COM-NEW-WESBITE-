Three Kings Day in Spain

By Anita Orłowska · 2 January 2026 · updated 26 October 2026 · 3 min read

In short: In Spain the main day for children's presents is 6 January, Three Kings Day (Día de Reyes), not Christmas. On the evening of 5 January almost every town holds a Cabalgata, a parade where the Kings Melchior, Gaspar and Balthazar throw sweets to the crowds. Children leave their shoes out, and in the morning they find presents (or coal if they have been naughty). Families share a ring-shaped roscón de Reyes with a hidden figurine and a bean. 6 January is a national holiday, and the winter sales start the next day.

For many people moving to Spain, the Christmas season seems to last surprisingly long. That is because it ends with the most exciting day of all for children: the arrival of the Three Kings.

Here is how Día de Reyes works and why it matters so much to Spanish families.

Who are the Three Kings?

The Reyes Magos, Melchior, Gaspar and Balthazar, are the wise men who, in the Christian story, followed a star to Bethlehem with gifts of gold, frankincense and myrrh. In Spain they bring presents to children on the night of 5 January, the feast of the Epiphany. In the weeks before, children write letters to their favourite king and post them in special mailboxes or hand them to the royal pages in shopping centres.

The Cabalgata de Reyes

On the evening of 5 January, towns of every size hold a Cabalgata: the Kings ride through the streets on decorated floats or camels, with bands, dancers and pages, throwing sweets to the children. Bring a bag for the sweets and arrive early for a good spot. Alcoy, inland from Alicante, has one of the oldest parades in Spain, and Madrid's and Málaga's are among the biggest.

Presents on 6 January

Before going to bed, children leave their shoes by the door or the window, often with water or something to eat for the Kings' camels. In the morning they find their presents. Naughty children, traditionally, get a lump of coal, which today is usually made of sugar. Many families now also give some presents at Christmas, but 6 January remains the big day.

Roscón de Reyes

The classic breakfast or afternoon treat on 6 January is the roscón de Reyes, a ring-shaped sweet bread flavoured with orange blossom and decorated with candied fruit, often filled with cream. Inside are two surprises: a small figurine, which brings luck to whoever finds it, and a dried bean, whose finder by tradition pays for next year's roscón. Bakeries take orders days in advance.

What it means for daily life

6 January is a national public holiday, so offices, banks and most shops are closed, and the evening before is busy with last-minute shopping and the parades. Schools return a day or two later. On 7 January the winter sales (rebajas) begin. For families moving to Spain, joining the Cabalgata is one of the easiest ways to feel part of the community. Frequently asked questions

When is Three Kings Day in Spain?

On 6 January. The parades take place on the evening of 5 January.

Is 6 January a public holiday in Spain?

Yes. It is a national holiday, and most shops, offices and banks are closed.

What is a roscón de Reyes?

A ring-shaped sweet bread with candied fruit, eaten on 6 January. It hides a figurine for good luck and a bean, whose finder pays for next year's cake.

Do Spanish children get presents at Christmas or on 6 January?

Traditionally on 6 January, from the Three Kings. Many families now give some presents at Christmas too.
