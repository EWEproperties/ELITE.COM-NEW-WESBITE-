Christmas in Spain: Traditions and the Festive Season

By Anita Orłowska · 16 December 2025 · updated 26 October 2026 · 3 min read

In short: Christmas in Spain is a long season: lights go up in late November, the El Gordo lottery on 22 December marks the real start, Christmas Eve (Nochebuena) is a late family dinner, and the season only ends on 6 January with the Three Kings. Nativity scenes (belenes) are more traditional than trees. Turrón, the almond nougat from Jijona near Alicante, is on every table. 28 December is the Spanish April Fools' Day. On the coast, it is often sunny enough for a walk on the beach.

Christmas on the Spanish coast is sunny walks under palm trees with twinkling lights, busy terraces and long family meals. It feels different from northern Europe, but it is just as festive.

Here is the Spanish Christmas calendar and the traditions behind it.

A season, not a day

Towns switch on their Christmas lights in late November or early December, and markets and ice rinks appear in the squares. The main holidays are 8 December (Immaculate Conception), 25 December, 1 January and 6 January. School holidays usually run from about 22 December to 7 January. Málaga's Calle Larios and Vigo are famous for their light shows.

Nativity scenes

In Spanish homes the belén (nativity scene) has long been more important than the tree. Town halls, churches and associations set up large ones, some with hundreds of figures, and visiting them is a family outing. Alicante's city centre has displayed giant nativity figures in recent years. In Catalonia you may spot the caganer, a cheeky figure hidden in the scene.

El Gordo, 22 December

The Christmas Lottery (Lotería de Navidad) is one of the oldest and biggest lotteries in the world. Families, colleagues and bars share tickets for months, and on the morning of 22 December the whole country listens as children from Madrid's San Ildefonso school sing out the winning numbers. For many Spaniards, this is when Christmas really begins.

Nochebuena and Christmas food

Christmas Eve (Nochebuena) is a late family dinner with seafood, fish, lamb or roast meat. Some people then go to Midnight Mass (Misa del Gallo). Christmas Day is a relaxed lunch. For dessert there is turrón, almond nougat, hard (Alicante) or soft (Jijona), both from the town of Jijona (Xixona) just inland from Alicante, plus marzipan and polvorones. Gifts traditionally arrive on 6 January, but Papá Noel now brings some on the 25th, and in the Basque Country and Catalonia there are local figures such as Olentzero and the Tió de Nadal.

Día de los Inocentes, 28 December

28 December is Spain's version of April Fools' Day. Newspapers and TV publish fake stories, and friends play small jokes on each other, such as sticking a paper figure on someone's back.

Christmas on the coast

On the Costa Blanca and Costa del Sol, December days are often sunny and around 17–18 °C. Many people take a walk on the seafront after lunch, and some brave a Christmas swim. It is one reason why so many northern Europeans now spend the whole winter here. See New Year in Spain and Three Kings Day.

Frequently asked questions

When does Christmas start in Spain?

Lights go up in late November or early December, but for many Spaniards the season really starts with the El Gordo lottery on 22 December. It ends on 6 January.

What do Spaniards eat at Christmas?

Seafood, fish and roast lamb or meat, followed by turrón, marzipan and polvorones.

Who brings presents in Spain at Christmas?

Traditionally the Three Kings on 6 January. Papá Noel now often brings some presents on 25 December too.

What is Día de los Inocentes?

28 December, the Spanish equivalent of April Fools' Day, with jokes and fake news stories.
