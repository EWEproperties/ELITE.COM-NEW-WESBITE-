NIE Number in Spain – What It Is and How to Get It

By Aleksandra Kobus · 22 December 2024 · updated 24 October 2026 · 3 min read

In short: The NIE is the identification and tax number every foreigner needs to buy property, open a bank account or sign contracts in Spain. You apply once and keep it for life. Apply at a national police station in Spain with an appointment, at a Spanish consulate in your country, or through a lawyer with a power of attorney. You need form EX-15, your passport, a reason such as a property reservation, and a fee of about €12.

The NIE is usually the first piece of paperwork for anyone buying in Spain. It is simple to get, but appointments can be scarce, so start early. Here is what it is and the three ways to get it.

What is the NIE?

The NIE (Número de Identidad de Extranjero) is a personal number given to foreigners who have financial, professional or social dealings in Spain. It works as your tax number and identifies you in every official document. It looks like a letter, seven digits and a check letter, for example X1234567L.

You get it once and it never changes, even if you later become resident. It is not a residence permit and does not give you the right to live in Spain.

When do you need it?

  • to buy, sell or rent property,
  • to pay taxes, including the yearly tax on your Spanish home,
  • to open a bank account or take out a mortgage,
  • to sign up for electricity, water and internet,
  • to buy a car or insurance,
  • to work or start a business in Spain.

Three ways to get it

1. In Spain, at the police

Book an appointment (cita previa) online for "asignación de NIE" at a national police station or foreigners' office, then attend in person. Appointments in popular coastal areas can be booked out for weeks, so look early and check often. The NIE certificate is usually issued the same day or within a few days.

2. At home, at a Spanish consulate

Spanish embassies and consulates abroad also handle NIE applications. Each sets its own appointment system, and the certificate is usually sent to you by email or post after a few weeks. Check the website of the consulate for your area.

3. Through a lawyer, with a power of attorney

If you cannot travel, you can sign a power of attorney (poder notarial) so a Spanish lawyer applies for you. Sign it at a Spanish consulate, or at a local notary and have it legalised with an apostille. A sworn translation may be needed if it is not in Spanish. The same power of attorney can also cover opening a bank account and signing your purchase.

Documents and fee

  • Form EX-15, filled in and signed.
  • Your passport or EU identity card, and a copy.
  • A document showing why you need the NIE, such as a reservation contract, a mortgage offer or a letter from your lawyer.
  • Proof of payment of the fee on form 790 code 012, about €12, paid at a Spanish bank or online.
  • Some offices and consulates also ask for a passport photo.

EU citizens who move to Spain for more than three months receive their NIE on the green registration certificate instead. Non-EU residents get it on their residence card (TIE).

Frequently asked questions

Does the NIE number expire?

No. The number is yours for life. Some older paper certificates had a validity date, but the number itself does not change or expire.

How long does it take to get an NIE?

At a police station in Spain, often the same day once you have an appointment; finding an appointment can take weeks. Through a consulate abroad, usually a few weeks.

How much does the NIE cost?

The official fee is about €12, paid with form 790 code 012. A lawyer will charge for their time if they apply for you.

Is the NIE the same as residency?

No. The NIE is an identification and tax number. Residency is a separate registration or permit.

Start with the NIE

Apply for your NIE as soon as you start looking seriously, so it never holds up a reservation or a signing. Ask us and we will put you in touch with a lawyer who can arrange your NIE, bank account and purchase under one power of attorney.
