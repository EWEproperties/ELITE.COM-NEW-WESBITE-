Home Insurance in Spain – A Guide for Owners and Non-Residents

By Anita Orłowska · 25 July 2025 · updated 24 October 2026 · 3 min read

In short: Home insurance (seguro de hogar) is not compulsory in Spain, except that a mortgaged home must have building damage cover. You can buy it from any insurer, not just from your bank. In an apartment the community's policy covers the building, so you mainly insure your contents, your own installations and your liability. Floods, storms and other extraordinary events are paid by the state-backed Consorcio de Compensación de Seguros through a small surcharge in every policy. An apartment policy usually costs a few hundred euros a year at most. Insure the rebuilding value, not the purchase price, and check the rules on empty homes and rentals.

A leak from the flat above, a storm, a break-in or a guest who slips by the pool: insurance is cheap compared with the cost of any of these, and for owners who live abroad it is close to essential.

Here is how home insurance works in Spain, what it covers and what to look for in the small print.

Types of cover

  • Building (continente): walls, roof, floors, fixed installations such as plumbing, wiring, fitted kitchen and bathrooms, and for villas also fences and the pool.
  • Contents (contenido): furniture, appliances, electronics, clothes and other belongings.
  • Home insurance (seguro de hogar): the usual combined policy for both, with third-party liability included.

In an apartment, the community of owners insures the building and common areas. You need cover for your contents, the parts of your home that are yours (installations, kitchen, flooring) and your liability towards neighbours. For a villa or detached house you insure everything yourself.

Most policies cover fire, water damage, theft and vandalism, broken glass, electrical damage and liability. Common extras are accidental damage (todo riesgo), emergency repairs, legal expenses, loss of rental income, rent default and cover for squatting (okupación).

Floods, storms and the Consorcio

Spain has a public fund for extraordinary risks, the Consorcio de Compensación de Seguros. Every home policy includes a small surcharge for it, and in return it pays for damage from floods, very strong winds, earthquakes and similar events, even if your insurer's policy excludes them.

You claim directly with the Consorcio, online or by phone. It pays according to the sums insured in your own policy, so being properly insured matters here too.

What affects the price

  • Rebuilding value of the home (not the purchase price, which includes the land),
  • value of contents, including jewellery or art declared separately,
  • location, for example near the sea or in flood-prone areas,
  • security: alarm, safe, gated complex, security grilles,
  • use: main home, holiday home, long-term or holiday rental,
  • extras and the excess you choose.

As a rough guide, a policy for an apartment often costs between about €150 and €400 a year, and a villa with pool and high-value contents more. Get two or three quotes.

Clauses to check

  • Underinsurance. If the sum insured is lower than the real value, the insurer pays only the same proportion of any claim. Many policies waive this up to a margin or offer a "first loss" option.
  • Empty homes. Some policies limit theft or water cover if the home stands empty for a long time, or require regular visits.
  • Rentals. Tell the insurer if you rent the home out. Standard policies often exclude holiday rentals, and some regions require liability insurance for tourist lets.
  • Mortgages. The bank needs proof of building cover and will be named as beneficiary, but you can choose the insurer. Bank policies are often more expensive.
  • Renewal. Policies renew automatically each year. To cancel, give written notice at least one month before the renewal date.
  • Language and service. Many insurers offer policies, claims lines and apps in English, and some in German.

Making a claim from abroad

Report damage as soon as possible, usually within seven days. Take photos, stop the damage from getting worse if you can, keep receipts for emergency repairs and report thefts to the police. A local key holder or property manager is useful here: they can let in the assessor and the repairer while you are away. Our guide to looking after your home explains how.

Frequently asked questions

Is home insurance compulsory in Spain?

No, except that a mortgaged home must have building damage insurance. In apartments, the community insures the building, but owners should still insure their contents and liability.

How much does home insurance cost in Spain?

For an apartment, often about €150–400 a year. Villas, high-value contents and extras such as holiday rental cover cost more.

Does my policy cover floods?

Flood damage is usually paid by the Consorcio de Compensación de Seguros, funded by a surcharge included in every home policy. You claim directly with the Consorcio.

Do I have to take my bank's insurance with a mortgage?

No. The bank needs building cover with itself as beneficiary, but you can buy it from any insurer.

Does home insurance cover holiday rentals?

Often not by default. Tell the insurer and add rental cover and liability insurance for guests.
