Benidorm: What's Worth Seeing – A Practical Guide

By Lucyna Gacek · 12 February 2025 · updated 26 October 2026 · 3 min read

In short: Benidorm is a city of about 75,000 residents in the middle of the Costa Blanca, around 55 minutes from Alicante airport. Famous for its skyline, it also has a charming old town with the Balcón del Mediterráneo viewpoint, two long Blue Flag beaches (Levante and Poniente), the Serra Gelada natural park, boat trips to Benidorm Island, theme parks such as Terra Mítica and Aqualandia, and golf nearby. It is busy all year, with a mild winter climate.

Benidorm is often called the Manhattan of the Mediterranean, and it has one of the highest numbers of high-rise buildings per inhabitant in the world. The plan behind it was deliberate: in the 1950s the mayor, Pedro Zaragoza, chose to build upwards rather than spread along the coast, leaving more space for beaches, gardens and views.

Beyond the towers you will find an old town, nature and some of the best-kept beaches in Spain. Here is what to see and some practical tips.

The old town

The old fishing village sits on a small headland between the two beaches. Its narrow streets are full of tapas bars, and Plaza de la Señoría and the church of San Jaime and Santa Ana, with its blue-tiled dome, are the heart of it. The church is home to the Virgen del Sufragio, whose festival in November is the city's biggest.

The Balcón del Mediterráneo

At the tip of the headland, on the site of the old castle, the white balustrade of the Balcón del Mediterráneo offers the classic view of both bays. Come at sunset, or early in the morning when it is quiet. Steps lead down to the small Mal Pas cove.

Levante, Poniente and the coves

  • Levante – about 2 km of sand by the busy centre, with bars, restaurants and water sports.
  • Poniente – about 3 km, wider and calmer, with an award-winning promenade and the city's residents on weekends.
  • Mal Pas – a small, sheltered cove below the old town.
  • Tío Ximo and L'Almadrava – small coves at the foot of the Serra Gelada.

Both main beaches fly the Blue Flag, have lifeguards in season and are cleaned every day. Read more in our guide to Costa Blanca beaches.

Serra Gelada and Benidorm Island

North-east of the city, the Serra Gelada natural park has cliffs over 400 m high and a coastal trail to the Punta del Cavall watchtower and to the lighthouse towards L'Albir, with views back over the skyline. Boats leave the port for Benidorm Island, a protected marine reserve good for snorkelling. Inland, the Puig Campana (1,406 m) is a challenging day hike.

Theme parks and golf

Benidorm has more theme parks than anywhere else in Spain: Terra Mítica (rides themed on ancient Mediterranean civilisations), Aqualandia water park, Mundomar with sea animals and Terra Natura zoo. Golfers have several courses within about 20 minutes, including Villaitana in Finestrat and the Puig Campana course.

Getting there and around

Alicante airport is about 60 km away, around 55 minutes by car on the AP-7. Direct buses run from the airport, and the TRAM line 1 links Benidorm with Alicante city centre in about 1 hour 10 minutes; line 9 continues north to Altea, Calpe and Dénia. In town, buses and walking are enough for most trips.

Buyers choose between apartments with sea views in the towers of Poniente and Levante, homes near the old town, or more space in Finestrat and La Nucía, 10–15 minutes away. Rental demand is strong all year. See our properties for sale in Benidorm.

Frequently asked questions

Is Benidorm worth visiting?

Yes. Beyond the skyline it has a pretty old town, two of Spain's best urban beaches, a natural park with coastal walks and plenty for families.

How far is Benidorm from Alicante airport?

About 60 km, around 55 minutes by car. Direct buses take about an hour.

Which beach is better in Benidorm, Levante or Poniente?

Levante is livelier and closer to the bars and old town. Poniente is wider, calmer and popular with families and residents.

Is Benidorm busy in winter?

Yes. It is one of the few Spanish resorts that stays busy all year, thanks to its mild winter and many long-stay visitors.
