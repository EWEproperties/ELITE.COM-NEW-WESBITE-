Spain by Car – Motorways, Roads and Driving from the UK

By Anita Orłowska · 14 April 2025 · updated 26 October 2026 · 4 min read

In short: From the UK you can take a ferry to Santander or Bilbao and drive about 8–10 hours to the Costa Blanca or Costa del Sol, or cross the Channel and drive around 1,900–2,200 km through France. Most Spanish motorways are free now, but some sections, including the AP-7 Alicante–Cartagena, Cartagena–Vera and the Costa del Sol toll road, still charge. Limits are 120 km/h on motorways, 90 on other roads and 50 or 30 in towns.

Driving to Spain lets you bring your own car, a full boot and the dog. It is a long trip, but well-planned it is also a pleasant one. This guide covers the best routes from the UK, Spain's road network and tolls, and the rules that differ from home.

Routes from the UK

  • Ferry to northern Spain: Brittany Ferries sails from Portsmouth and Plymouth to Santander and from Portsmouth to Bilbao, with crossings of about 24–32 hours. From Santander it is about 820 km (8–9 hours) to Alicante and about 950 km (9–10 hours) to Málaga, mostly on free motorways via Madrid.
  • Channel crossing and France: from Calais or the Eurotunnel it is about 1,900 km to Alicante and 2,200 km to Marbella. Most people stop for one or two nights, for example near Bordeaux or Lyon. French motorways have tolls, often more than €100 one way.

Since 2021 UK cars need a "UK" sticker (or the UK symbol on the number plate) and no longer need a green card for insurance. Check that your policy covers Europe and breakdown abroad.

Spain's main roads

Spain has one of the largest motorway networks in Europe. Autovías (A-) are free dual carriageways; autopistas (AP-) were built as toll roads, but many concessions have ended. The key routes for the coasts are:

  • A-7 / AP-7 Mediterranean corridor: from the French border along the coast through Valencia, Alicante, Murcia and Málaga.
  • A-31 and A-3: from Madrid to Alicante and Valencia.
  • A-4, A-44 and A-45: from Madrid to Málaga via Córdoba or Granada.
  • N-332 and N-340: the old coastal roads, slower but scenic, through towns on the Costa Blanca and Costa del Sol.

Which motorways still have tolls?

Since 2018–2021, the AP-7 from the French border to Alicante, the AP-1, AP-2 and AP-4 Sevilla–Cádiz have become free. In 2026 the main tolls near our areas are:

  • AP-7 Alicante–Cartagena (a free alternative is the A-7 inland).
  • AP-7 Cartagena–Vera, free at night from midnight to 6:00.
  • AP-7 Málaga–Guadiaro on the Costa del Sol (the free A-7 runs alongside, with more traffic).

You pay at toll booths by card or cash, or with an electronic tag (Via-T), which some UK and French tags also support. Toll prices rose by up to about 4.7% in January 2026.

Spanish road rules to know

  • Speed limits: 120 km/h on motorways, 90 km/h on other roads, 50 km/h in towns and 30 km/h on single-lane urban streets.
  • Alcohol: limit 0.5 g/l (0.25 mg/l in breath), 0.3 g/l for new drivers. Tests are frequent.
  • Equipment: a reflective vest within reach, and you must wear it if you get out on the road. Spanish cars must carry a connected V-16 warning light from 2026; foreign-registered cars may still use two warning triangles.
  • Phones: holding a phone while driving is not allowed, even when stopped at lights.
  • Cyclists: leave at least 1.5 m when overtaking; you may cross a solid line to do so if it is safe.
  • Fines: foreign drivers can be asked to pay on the spot. Our guide to traffic fines in Spain lists the amounts.

Low-emission zones

Larger Spanish cities, including Madrid, Barcelona, Valencia and Málaga, have low-emission zones (ZBE) that limit older cars. Rules and the need to register a foreign car differ by city, so check the city's website before driving into the centre. Coastal towns are mostly not affected yet.

If you move to Spain with your car

As a resident you can drive a UK-registered car in Spain only for a limited time; after that it must be registered in Spain (matriculación), which involves a technical inspection and registration tax. UK licence holders who become resident must exchange their licence for a Spanish one within six months. See our guide to moving to Spain step by step for the details.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to drive from the UK to Spain?

With a ferry to Santander or Bilbao, about 8–10 hours of driving to the Costa Blanca or Costa del Sol. Via the Channel and France, around 20–24 hours of driving, usually over two or three days.

Are Spanish motorways toll roads?

Most are free now. Some sections still charge, including the AP-7 Alicante–Cartagena, Cartagena–Vera and Málaga–Guadiaro on the Costa del Sol.

What is the speed limit on Spanish motorways?

120 km/h, unless signs show a lower limit. Other roads are 90 km/h and towns 50 or 30 km/h.

Do I need a green card to drive a UK car in Spain?

No, not since 2021. You need a UK sticker or UK on the plate, your licence, registration document and valid insurance.
