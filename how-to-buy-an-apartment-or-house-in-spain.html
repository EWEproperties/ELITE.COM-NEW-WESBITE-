How to Buy an Apartment or House in Spain – Step by Step

By Anita Orłowska · 15 December 2024 · updated 24 October 2026 · 4 min read

In short: Buying in Spain usually takes 6–10 weeks for a resale home: get your NIE and a Spanish bank account, reserve the property, let your lawyer check it, sign a deposit contract (usually 10%) and complete at the notary. Budget about 11–13% on top of the price for taxes and fees. For an off-plan new build, the law requires your stage payments to be protected by a bank guarantee or insurance.

Buying a home in Spain is not harder than at home, it is just different. A notary, a public land registry and a deposit contract are at the heart of it. This guide walks you through each step and the costs, so you know what to expect.

Step 1: Prepare

  • Set your budget, including about 11–13% for taxes and fees.
  • Get your NIE, the foreigner's tax number. You need it to buy, pay taxes and sign up for utilities. You can apply at a Spanish consulate at home or in Spain, or give a lawyer power of attorney to get it for you.
  • Open a Spanish bank account. You will pay the deposit, taxes and bills from it.
  • If you need a mortgage, ask a bank or broker for an approval in principle before you start viewing. Non-residents can usually borrow 60–70% of the price.
  • Choose an independent lawyer who works for you, not for the seller or the developer.

Step 2: Reserve and sign the deposit contract

When you find the right home, you usually pay a reservation fee (often €3,000–€10,000) to take it off the market while your lawyer checks it. Your lawyer then checks the Nota Simple, debts, licences, IBI and community fees.

Next comes the private contract, usually a contrato de arras, with a deposit of about 10% of the price. It fixes the price and the date for signing at the notary. The most common type is arras penitenciales: if you pull out, you lose the deposit; if the seller pulls out, they must pay you back double.

For a new build bought off-plan, you sign a purchase contract with the developer and pay in stages during construction. Spanish law requires these payments to be protected by a bank guarantee or insurance for each payment, so you get your money back if the home is not finished. Ask for the guarantee each time you pay.

Step 3: Complete at the notary

Ownership passes when you and the seller sign the escritura pública de compraventa (the deed of sale) before a Spanish notary. You pay the rest of the price at the same time, by bank transfer or banker's cheque, and get the keys. The notary checks identities and the registry, reads out the deed and records how the money was paid. Cash is not used.

You need your passport, your NIE, the bank details of your payments and, with a mortgage, the bank's representative at the same signing.

Cannot travel? You can give a lawyer a power of attorney (poder notarial) to sign for you. Sign it at a Spanish consulate, or at a notary at home with an apostille.

Step 4: After the signing

  • Your lawyer or the notary's office pays the purchase tax, usually within a month, and registers the deed at the Land Registry.
  • Transfer the water, electricity and internet into your name.
  • Set up direct debits for IBI and community fees.
  • Insure the home.
  • As a non-resident, file your yearly non-resident tax return for the property.

Common mistakes

  • Paying a deposit before a lawyer has checked the property.
  • Using the seller's or developer's lawyer instead of your own.
  • Not asking for the bank guarantee on off-plan payments.
  • Forgetting that extensions, pools or terraces may not be licensed.
  • Not budgeting for taxes, fees and the yearly costs of owning.
  • Assuming you can rent the home to tourists without checking the licence rules.

Frequently asked questions

Can foreigners buy property in Spain?

Yes. There are no restrictions on EU or non-EU citizens buying property. You need an NIE and, in practice, a Spanish bank account.

How much are the buying costs in Spain?

About 11–13% of the price: 10% VAT plus 1.2–1.4% stamp duty on a new build, or 7–11% transfer tax on a resale home, plus about 1.5–2% for notary, registry and lawyer.

How long does buying a property in Spain take?

Usually 6–10 weeks for a resale home paid in cash, a little longer with a mortgage. Off-plan homes complete when the building is finished.

Can I buy a property in Spain without travelling there?

Yes. You can give a lawyer a power of attorney, signed at a Spanish consulate or at a notary at home with an apostille.
