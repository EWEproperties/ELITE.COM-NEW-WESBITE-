Costa Blanca Beaches You Must Visit

By Lucyna Gacek · 26 September 2025 · updated 26 October 2026 · 4 min read

In short: The Costa Blanca has more than 200 km of coast with three kinds of beach. Long sandy beaches with promenades and lifeguards (San Juan and Postiguet in Alicante, Levante and Poniente in Benidorm, La Fossa in Calpe, El Arenal in Jávea); small rocky coves with clear water for snorkelling in the north (Granadella, Portitxol, Moraig, La Fustera, L'Albir); and quieter natural beaches backed by dunes and pine woods in the south (Guardamar, La Mata). Alicante province has one of the highest numbers of Blue Flag beaches in Spain.

The Costa Blanca runs from Dénia in the north to Pilar de la Horadada in the south. In the north, mountains meet the sea and the coast breaks into cliffs and coves. South of Alicante it flattens out into long sandy beaches, dunes and salt lakes. With around 300 sunny days a year, the beach season here is far longer than the summer holidays: many residents swim from May to October and walk on the sand all winter.

Here are the beaches we recommend most often to clients, grouped by type, with a few practical tips.

Sandy beaches for comfort and families

These are wide, gently sloping beaches with lifeguards in season, showers, sunbeds, beach bars and a promenade behind. Most fly the Blue Flag, which means water quality, safety and services are checked every year.

  • Playa de San Juan (Alicante) – about 7 km of fine golden sand that continues into El Campello. Calm, shallow water, good for families, and popular for windsurfing and paddle boarding.
  • El Postiguet (Alicante) – the city beach below Santa Bárbara Castle, next to the Explanada and the marina. Ideal for a swim between a walk in the old town and lunch.
  • La Fossa and Arenal-Bol (Calpe) – two sandy beaches on either side of the Peñón de Ifach, with an easy, flat promenade and good access for people with reduced mobility.
  • El Arenal (Jávea) – Jávea's only big sandy beach, lined with restaurants and very popular with families.

Benidorm: Levante and Poniente

Benidorm's two big bays are among the best-kept urban beaches in Spain. Levante (about 2 km) is lively, with bars and the old town at one end. Poniente (about 3 km) is calmer and wider, with a modern, award-winning promenade. Both are cleaned daily and have lifeguards in season. The small Mal Pas cove between them is sheltered and good for children.

Coves among the rocks: the calas of the north

North of Benidorm the coast becomes steep, and the beaches turn into small calas: coves of pebbles or sand between cliffs, with very clear water. They are the best places on the Costa Blanca for snorkelling.

  • Cala Granadella (Jávea) – often voted one of the most beautiful beaches in Spain, a pebble cove surrounded by pine woods. In summer access by car is limited, so come early or by shuttle.
  • Cala Portitxol / La Barraca (Jávea) – white fishermen's huts, a small island and calm water.
  • Cala del Moraig (Benitachell) – turquoise water under high cliffs, with the sea cave Cova dels Arcs at one end.
  • Cala La Fustera (Benissa) – a small sandy cove, sheltered and family-friendly.
  • Playa del Albir (L'Alfàs del Pi) – a long pebble beach at the foot of the Serra Gelada natural park, with a calm promenade.

Further south, Villajoyosa has a sandy town beach in front of its famous colourful houses. See our homes in Villajoyosa and Altea.

Tip: wear water shoes on pebble beaches, and bring a mask: the sea grass meadows (posidonia) are full of fish.

Natural beaches of the south: dunes and pine woods

South of Alicante the landscape changes. In Guardamar del Segura, several kilometres of beach are backed by dunes and a pine forest planted over a century ago, with shady paths for walking and cycling. Between Torrevieja and Guardamar, La Mata beach borders the La Mata natural park and its salt lake. Along Orihuela Costa, small sandy beaches alternate with rocky points and a coastal path, from Punta Prima to Mil Palmeras.

Practical tips

  • Best season: the sea is warmest in August and September, at around 25–27 °C. June and early October are warm and much quieter.
  • Lifeguards: most town beaches have lifeguards from June to September. Check the flag: green means safe, yellow means take care, red means no swimming.
  • Parking: in July and August, arrive before 10 am at popular coves, or take the bus or a shuttle.
  • Rules: many towns ban dogs, smoking or barbecues on the main beaches in summer; some have special dog beaches. Look for the signs at the entrance.
  • Naturist beaches: naturism is legal on Spanish beaches, and some quieter beaches and coves are known as naturist-friendly.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best beach on the Costa Blanca?

It depends on what you like. For families, San Juan in Alicante, Levante and Poniente in Benidorm and La Fossa in Calpe. For clear water and scenery, Cala Granadella in Jávea and Cala del Moraig in Benitachell.

Are Costa Blanca beaches sandy or rocky?

Both. South of Alicante beaches are mostly long and sandy. North of Benidorm there are more pebble beaches and rocky coves.

When can you swim on the Costa Blanca?

Most people swim from May or June to October. The sea is warmest in August and September, at around 25–27 °C.

Do Costa Blanca beaches have Blue Flags?

Yes. Alicante province has one of the highest numbers of Blue Flag beaches in Spain. The list is renewed every year.
