IBI Property Tax in Spain 2026 – What You Will Pay

By Anita Orłowska · 24 February 2026 · updated 24 October 2026 · 4 min read

In short: IBI is the yearly local property tax every owner in Spain pays to the town hall, resident or not. It is a percentage of the cadastral value (valor catastral), which is usually well below the market price. Towns set their own rate between 0.4% and 1.1%. In 2026 the urban rate is 0.4% in Torrevieja, about 0.62% in Alicante, 0.695% in Orihuela, 0.82% in Benidorm and 0.65% in Marbella. A typical apartment on the Costa Blanca pays a few hundred euros a year. Whoever owns the home on 1 January owes that year's IBI.

When you buy in Spain, the purchase taxes get all the attention. IBI is smaller, but you pay it every year for as long as you own the home, so it belongs in any honest budget.

Here is how IBI works, what it costs in the main towns on the Costa Blanca and Costa del Sol in 2026, and what to check before you buy.

What is IBI?

IBI (Impuesto sobre Bienes Inmuebles) is an annual tax on owning real estate, collected by the town hall (ayuntamiento). It applies to apartments, houses, plots and commercial premises.

The tax is tied to the property, not to the person. Every owner pays it, whether resident or non-resident, and whether the home is lived in, rented out or empty.

IBI is only one of the yearly costs of owning. Non-residents also pay non-resident income tax on the home, and some owners pay wealth tax. Our guide to property and wealth taxes in Spain covers them all.

How is IBI calculated?

IBI does not depend on what you paid for the home. It is based on the cadastral value (valor catastral), an official value set by the Spanish land registry office (Catastro) for every property. It is usually far below the market price, often less than half.

The town hall applies its own rate to that value:

IBI = cadastral value × town rate

By law, the rate for urban property must be between 0.4% and 1.1% in most towns. Many towns also offer discounts, for example for large families, homes with solar panels or new homes owned by a developer, and some give a small discount if you pay by direct debit.

You can find the cadastral value on the last IBI bill or in the online land registry (Sede Electrónica del Catastro).

Because cadastral values are low, the real bills are modest. An average home pays about €220 a year in Torrevieja, €315 in Alicante and €495 in Benidorm. In Marbella, where cadastral values are higher, the average is close to €1,000.

Rates change, so check the current tax ordinance (ordenanza fiscal) of the town before you buy, or ask us for the last bill of the home you like.

Who pays and when?

IBI is due from whoever owns the home on 1 January. If you buy in March, the seller legally owes that year's tax, but most purchase contracts split it between buyer and seller by the number of days each owns the home.

In the province of Alicante, most towns collect IBI through the provincial tax office SUMA. In 2026 the payment period there ran from late July to early October. Other provinces and towns have their own dates. The easiest way, especially if you live abroad, is a direct debit from a Spanish bank account.

Check for unpaid IBI before you buy

Unpaid IBI stays with the property. If the seller has not paid the last years, the town can claim it from the new owner. Your lawyer should ask for the last IBI receipt and a certificate that there is no debt before the signing.

Can IBI go up?

Yes, for two reasons:

  • The town changes its rate. This is decided each year in the tax ordinance.
  • The cadastral value is updated. The Catastro can revise the values in a whole town, or the State can apply a yearly update coefficient that the town has asked for. Your IBI then rises even if the market price of your home has not.

Rises are usually gradual, but over 10–15 years they add up. When you compare two homes, look at the actual bill and the cadastral value, not just the rate.

IBI and rental income

If you rent out your home, IBI is a cost of the rental. Residents of Spain and non-residents from the EU or EEA can deduct it, together with other costs, from their rental income. Non-residents from outside the EU and EEA, including the UK, are taxed on gross rental income and cannot deduct it.

The cadastral value also matters for other taxes. Non-residents pay income tax on an imputed income of 1.1% of the cadastral value (2% if it has not been revised recently) for every year the home is not rented out. Our guide to resident and non-resident tax explains the difference.

Frequently asked questions

How much is IBI on an apartment in Spain?

It depends on the cadastral value and the town. On the Costa Blanca, a typical apartment pays roughly €200–600 a year. In Marbella and other high-value areas it is often €1,000 or more.

Do non-residents pay IBI?

Yes. IBI is paid by every owner, resident or not. Non-residents also pay non-resident income tax on the home every year.

Who pays IBI in the year I buy?

Legally, the owner on 1 January. In practice most purchase contracts split that year's IBI between seller and buyer by the days each owns the home.

Where do I find the cadastral value of a property?

On the IBI bill, or in the Sede Electrónica del Catastro using the cadastral reference. The owner can download a certificate with the value.

What happens if the seller has not paid IBI?

The debt stays with the property, so the town can claim it from you. Ask for the last receipt and a no-debt certificate before signing.
