Retiring in Spain – A Practical Guide

By Aleksandra Kobus · 16 June 2025 · updated 24 October 2026 · 5 min read

In short: A couple who own their home outright can live comfortably on the Costa Blanca for about €1,700–€2,200 a month. EU citizens only need to register as residents. British and other non-EU retirees usually need the non-lucrative visa, which asks for about €28,800 a year in income for one person in 2026. A state pensioner can often use an S1 form to join the Spanish public health system. Rent before you buy, and get tax advice on where your pension will be taxed.

Retiring in Spain means more sun, the sea close by and a slower pace of life. It also means planning: your budget, your residence papers, healthcare and tax. This guide brings it all together, step by step.

Why retire in Spain?

  • Climate. Long, warm summers and mild winters let you spend time outdoors all year, which is good for body and mind.
  • Cost of living. Everyday life on the coast is cheaper than in the UK, Germany, France or the Benelux countries, especially eating out, fresh food and local services.
  • Healthcare. Spain's public health system is well regarded, and the coast has many private clinics with English- and German-speaking doctors.
  • Community. On the Costa Blanca and Costa del Sol you will find large international communities, clubs and associations, which makes settling in much easier.

The best places to retire

  • Costa Blanca. Towns such as Altea, Jávea, Calpe, Villajoyosa and Torrevieja offer good value, golf, many international residents and Alicante airport within an hour.
  • Costa del Sol. Marbella, Estepona, Fuengirola, Benalmádena and Nerja combine the mildest winters in mainland Spain with excellent private healthcare and Málaga airport.
  • Costa Cálida. The Murcia coast around the Mar Menor is quieter and among the most affordable.

Before choosing, spend a few weeks there outside the summer season. Some resorts are lively in August and very quiet in January. For year-round living, closeness to a health centre, shops, a pharmacy and public transport matters more than being right on the beach.

How much money do you need?

It depends on the area, your home and your lifestyle. As a guide, for a couple who own their home outright on the Costa Blanca:

  • about €1,300–€1,600 a month for a simple lifestyle,
  • about €1,700–€2,200 a month for a comfortable one.

That covers food, utilities, phone and internet, transport, insurance, community fees and local taxes, and some eating out. If you rent, add the rent: a long-term apartment on the Costa Blanca often costs €700–€1,000 a month, and more on the Costa del Sol. Keep an emergency fund for repairs and unexpected costs.

Residency: EU citizens and UK or other non-EU citizens

EU, EEA and Swiss citizens

You do not need a visa. If you stay longer than three months, register at the foreigners' office or police station to get your registration certificate. You will need proof of enough income (your pension) and health cover.

British and other non-EU citizens

Since Brexit, British citizens are treated as non-EU nationals. For stays longer than 90 days in any 180, you need a visa. Most retirees use the non-lucrative residence visa, which you apply for at the Spanish consulate in your home country. In 2026 you need to show income or savings of at least about €28,800 a year for yourself, plus about €7,200 for each family member, and private health insurance with no co-payments. The visa does not allow you to work in Spain.

British citizens who were already living in Spain before 2021 keep their rights under the Withdrawal Agreement and should check their existing status first.

Everyone also needs an NIE, the foreigner's tax number, to buy property, open a bank account or sign contracts. An NIE is not a residence permit.

Healthcare

If you receive a state pension from an EU country or the UK, you can usually get an S1 form from your home country. You register it with the Spanish social security office, and you then have access to the Spanish public health system on the same terms as Spanish pensioners. UK pensioners apply through NHS Overseas Healthcare Services.

An S1 does not usually replace the private insurance required for the non-lucrative visa application, so check the rules with the consulate. Many retirees also keep private insurance for faster access to specialists. Bring your medical records and a list of your medicines, and register with your local health centre soon after you arrive.

Pensions and tax

If you spend more than 183 days a year in Spain, or your main home and family are here, you are usually tax resident in Spain and must declare your worldwide income here. Owning a home in Spain does not by itself make you tax resident.

Where your pension is taxed depends on the type of pension and the tax treaty between Spain and your home country. Under the UK–Spain treaty, UK state and private pensions are generally taxed in Spain, while UK government service pensions (for example for former civil servants, teachers or police) stay taxable in the UK. Your pension can be paid into a Spanish or a home bank account.

Inheritance tax is regional. The Valencian Community and Andalusia both give spouses and children a large reduction. Get advice from a tax adviser who knows both countries before you move.

Rent first or buy?

Renting for six months to a year is often wise if you do not know the area well. You find out whether you like the climate in winter, the language, transport and the rhythm of daily life.

If you already know where you want to be, buying gives you stability and protects you from rising rents. When you choose a home for retirement, look at:

  • distance to a health centre, hospital, pharmacy and shops,
  • public transport and parking,
  • a lift and step-free access,
  • community fees and running costs,
  • noise and the feel of the neighbourhood,
  • a spare room for visiting family.

Frequently asked questions

Can British citizens retire in Spain?

Yes. Since Brexit most need a visa for stays over 90 days. The non-lucrative residence visa is the usual route for retirees: in 2026 you need about €28,800 a year in income for one person, plus private health insurance.

How much do I need to retire in Spain?

A couple who own their home outright on the Costa Blanca can live comfortably on about €1,700–€2,200 a month. Add rent if you do not own your home.

Can I use Spanish public healthcare as a pensioner?

Usually yes. If you receive a state pension from an EU country or the UK, you can register an S1 form in Spain and use the public health system like a Spanish pensioner.

Will my pension be taxed in Spain?

If you are tax resident in Spain, most pensions are taxed here. Government service pensions are often still taxed in the country that pays them. It depends on the tax treaty, so get advice.
