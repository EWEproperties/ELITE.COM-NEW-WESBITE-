Traffic Fines in Spain – What They Cost and How to Pay Less

By Aleksandra Kobus · 20 March 2026 · updated 24 October 2026 · 4 min read

In short: Most serious traffic offences in Spain cost €200, such as holding a phone, not wearing a seatbelt or running a red light, and drink-driving costs €500 or €1,000. Speeding fines run from €100 to €600. Fines reach you even in a rental or foreign car, and if you pay within 20 days you get 50% off, but you give up the right to appeal.

Getting a traffic fine in Spain is surprisingly easy. A glance at your phone, a few km/h too fast on an empty road or a car parked in the wrong spot is enough. Here is what the most common fines cost, how the checks work and how to pay less if you get one.

Why fines catch so many drivers out

Spain's road checks are largely automatic. Fixed and mobile speed cameras, average speed zones, cameras at traffic lights and low-emission zones in the bigger cities record offences without a police officer stopping you. The traffic authority (DGT) also uses helicopters, drones and unmarked cars.

So many drivers only learn about a fine weeks later, by post, by email from the rental company or through the DGT's electronic mailbox.

Speed limits and speeding fines

The general limits are:

  • 120 km/h on motorways (autopistas and autovías),
  • 90 km/h on other roads,
  • 50 km/h on town streets with two or more lanes in each direction, 30 km/h on streets with one lane each way, and 20 km/h on streets where pavement and road are at the same level.

Speeding fines depend on how far over the limit you were. Going up to 20 km/h too fast on a 50 km/h street, or up to 30 km/h too fast on a motorway, costs €100 and no points. Higher speeds cost €300 to €600 and 2 to 6 points. Driving more than 60 km/h over the limit in town, or more than 80 km/h over on other roads, is a criminal offence.

Alcohol and drugs

The limit is 0.5 g/l in the blood (0.25 mg/l in breath). For drivers with less than two years' experience and professional drivers it is 0.3 g/l (0.15 mg/l in breath).

  • Over the limit: €500 and 4 points.
  • More than twice the limit, or a repeat offence within a year: €1,000 and 6 points.
  • More than 1.2 g/l (0.60 mg/l in breath) is a criminal offence, with a fine, community service or prison, and loss of the licence.

Driving with drugs in your system costs €1,000 and 6 points. Refusing a test is a criminal offence.

Rental cars and foreign cars

If you are caught in a rental car, the fine goes first to the rental company. It gives your details to the authorities, who send you the fine, often weeks later. Most companies also charge an admin fee to your card.

A fine also reaches you at home if you drive your own foreign car. EU countries share vehicle owner data for the main road safety offences, such as speeding, drink-driving, not wearing a seatbelt and using a phone, so a camera fine follows you home.

If you have no address in Spain and are stopped by the police, you will normally have to pay on the spot, with the 50% discount. If you cannot pay, the car can be held until you do.

How to pay and get 50% off

Most fines are halved if you pay within 20 calendar days of receiving the notice. If you take the discount, you accept the fine and can no longer appeal it, and the points are still deducted.

You can pay:

  • online on the DGT website or the miDGT app,
  • at a bank or post office using the notice,
  • at a DGT office.

Fines for parking and other town offences are paid to the town hall, usually online. If you own a Spanish car or live in Spain, sign up to the DGT's electronic mailbox (DEV) so you receive notices quickly and do not miss the discount.

Frequently asked questions

How much is a speeding fine in Spain?

From €100 for going slightly over the limit up to €600 for the most serious cases, with up to 6 points. Going more than 60 km/h over the limit in town or 80 km/h over elsewhere is a criminal offence.

Will a Spanish traffic fine reach me at home?

Yes. EU countries exchange vehicle owner data for speeding, drink-driving, seatbelt and phone offences, so a fine can be sent to your home address in another EU country.

How do I get the 50% discount?

Pay within 20 calendar days of receiving the notice. You give up the right to appeal, and any points are still deducted.

What is the alcohol limit for drivers in Spain?

0.5 g/l in the blood or 0.25 mg/l in breath. For new drivers (less than two years) and professional drivers it is 0.3 g/l, or 0.15 mg/l in breath.

Drive relaxed

Spanish roads are good and the coast is a pleasure to drive. Keep your phone out of reach, watch the speed limits in towns and check where you can park, and fines will rarely trouble you.
