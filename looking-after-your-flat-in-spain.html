Looking After Your Home in Spain While You Are Away

By Aleksandra Kobus · 21 July 2025 · updated 24 October 2026 · 3 min read

In short: Most holiday homes in Spain stand empty for months. A key holding or home-care service visits regularly to check for leaks, damp, power cuts and break-ins, airs the rooms, runs the taps and air conditioning, collects the post and acts as your contact for the alarm company and the community. Simple fortnightly checks cost from a few dozen euros a month; full rental management usually costs a percentage of the income. Prepare the home before you leave, pay bills by direct debit and check that your insurance covers long absences.

A home by the sea needs attention even when nobody lives there. Humidity, storms, power cuts and blocked drains do more damage to empty homes than people expect, and a full postbox tells everyone the owner is away.

Here is what a home-care service does, what it costs and how to set up your home so you can leave it with peace of mind.

What a home-care service does

A key holder or home-care company keeps a set of keys and checks the home on a fixed schedule, usually weekly or every two weeks. A typical visit includes:

  • checking doors, windows, shutters and the alarm,
  • looking for leaks, damp, mould and signs of insects,
  • running taps and flushing toilets so drains do not dry out and smell,
  • airing the rooms and running the air conditioning briefly,
  • checking the fridge, fuse box and water heater after power cuts,
  • collecting and forwarding post,
  • a short report with photos.

Extra services are usually available: checks after storms, cleaning and fresh linen before you arrive, filling the fridge, garden and pool care, meeting tradespeople and handling small repairs. The company can also be the contact for your alarm and for the community administrator, which matters because alarm companies call a local key holder, not you.

Prices vary a lot by area, size and scope, so ask two or three companies for a written offer. The cost is small compared with the bill for a leak that runs for two months unnoticed.

Before you leave: a checklist

  1. Water: close the main tap, or at least the one to the water heater, unless a garden or pool system needs it.
  2. Electricity: leave the fridge open and switched off if empty, unplug devices, keep the alarm and any router or cameras on.
  3. Damp: leave interior doors open, consider a dehumidifier on a timer, and do not leave wet towels or food.
  4. Outside: bring in or tie down terrace furniture and close awnings before storm season.
  5. Bills: pay utilities, community fees and IBI by direct debit from a Spanish account.
  6. Insurance: read the policy. Some exclude damage if the home is empty for long periods or ask for regular visits.
  7. Smart devices: water-leak sensors and a smart plug for the router are cheap and useful. Cameras may only film your own home, not communal areas or the street.

A home that looks lived in and is visited regularly is also the best protection against squatters. Our guide to squatters in Spain explains the risk in more detail.

Home care or rental management?

If you want the home to earn money while you are away, a rental manager goes further: advertising, bookings, guest check-in, cleaning between stays, guest registration with the police and the rental taxes. That only works with a valid tourist licence, or as a long-term or seasonal let.

If you mainly use the home yourself, simple home care is enough. Many owners combine both: rental in the summer peak, home care the rest of the year.

How to choose a company

  • A registered business with a written contract and clear prices.
  • Liability insurance and a safe system for keys.
  • Reports with date-stamped photos after each visit.
  • Reachable by phone or messaging in your language.
  • References from owners in the same complex or area.

Frequently asked questions

How often should an empty holiday home be checked?

Every one or two weeks is common, plus a visit after storms or power cuts. Villas with pools and gardens may need weekly visits.

How much does key holding cost in Spain?

Basic key holding with regular checks starts at a few dozen euros a month for an apartment. Villas and extra services cost more.

Does my home insurance cover a home that stands empty?

Not always. Some policies limit cover after a period of absence or require regular visits. Check the conditions and keep the visit reports.

Should I turn off the water when I leave?

Usually yes, at least the main tap or the water heater supply, unless an irrigation or pool system needs water.

Can a home-care company also rent out my home?

Many offer rental management too, but holiday lets need a tourist licence. Without one, only long-term or seasonal lets are allowed.
