Buying a New Build in Spain: The Process Step by Step

By Aleksandra Kobus · 9 January 2026 · updated 25 October 2026 · 4 min read

In short: Buying from a developer in Spain usually takes six steps: reservation, NIE and bank account, private purchase contract, staged payments during construction, the deed at the notary and handover of the keys. Every payment made before completion must, by law, go into a special account and be covered by a bank guarantee or insurance, so you get it back with interest if the home is not finished. After handover the law gives you warranties of 1 year for finishes, 3 years for habitability defects and 10 years for the structure. New homes in Spain are sold finished, with kitchen, bathrooms and usually air conditioning.

More and more homes on the Spanish coast are sold straight from the developer, often before they are built. The process is safe when you know the steps and the protections the law gives you. This guide explains both. For the pros and cons of new versus resale, see new build or resale.

What you get with a new build

  • A finished home: new homes are handed over ready to live in, with a fitted kitchen, finished bathrooms, flooring and, on the coast, usually air conditioning.
  • Low running costs: good insulation, efficient heating and cooling, and often solar panels. New homes usually have an A or B energy rating.
  • Shared facilities: many complexes have pools, gardens, gyms and secure parking.
  • Choice: if you buy early, you can often choose the floor, the view and some finishes.

The price per square metre is usually higher than for a resale home, and you wait for completion if you buy off-plan.

The process step by step

  1. Choose the home and check the developer and the project (see below).
  2. Reservation (contrato de reserva): you pay a deposit, often €6,000 to €20,000, which takes the home off the market. Check whether it is refundable.
  3. NIE and bank account: you need a NIE number and a Spanish bank account.
  4. Private purchase contract (contrato privado de compraventa), usually within a month. It sets the price, the payment plan, the delivery date and the specifications. You pay the first instalment.
  5. Staged payments during construction. A common plan is 20 to 40% before completion and the rest at the notary, but plans vary.
  6. Deed and keys: when the building is finished and licensed, you sign the escritura at the notary, pay the balance and receive the keys. The deed is then registered in your name.

Buying costs for a new home are 10% VAT plus stamp duty (AJD), which is 1.4% in the Valencian Community and 1.2% in Andalusia, plus notary, registry and legal fees. See how to buy in Spain.

How your money is protected

Spanish law protects buyers who pay before a home is finished:

  • Guarantee of payments: all money you pay before completion must go into a special account and be covered by a bank guarantee (aval bancario) or an insurance policy. If the home is not built or not delivered on time, you get your money back with legal interest. Ask for an individual guarantee for each payment.
  • Warranties after handover under the Building Act (LOE): 1 year for finishing defects, 3 years for defects that affect habitability, such as damp or poor insulation, and 10 years for the structure, covered by compulsory ten-year insurance (seguro decenal).

What to check before you sign

  • The developer: how long they have been building, finished projects you can visit and their financial health.
  • The building licence (licencia de obras) and that the land is registered in the developer's name.
  • The guarantee: the bank or insurer and the special account where you will pay.
  • The contract: price with or without VAT, payment plan, delivery date and what happens if it is late, the specifications (memoria de calidades), the plans and the parking or storage included.
  • Future costs: estimated community fees and IBI property tax.

An independent lawyer should check the contract before you sign. We work with lawyers who do this for our clients.

Completion and handover

When the building is finished, the developer obtains the end-of-works certificate and the first-occupation licence or declaration from the town hall. Then you can sign the deed.

  • Inspect the home before or at handover and write a snagging list of anything that is unfinished or faulty. The developer must fix it.
  • Collect the documents: the energy certificate, the building book (libro del edificio) and the warranty papers for appliances.
  • Connect the utilities in your name: electricity, water and internet. We help with this.

Renting out a new home

New homes are easy to rent: tenants like low bills, modern design and shared facilities, and the owner has few repairs for years. For holiday letting, check the regional tourist licence and the complex's own rules before you buy, as some communities now ban it. See short-term rental rules and long-term rental.

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to buy off-plan in Spain?

Yes, if your payments are protected. By law every payment before completion must go into a special account and be covered by a bank guarantee or insurance, so you get it back with interest if the home is not delivered.

How much do I pay before completion?

It depends on the developer. A reservation deposit of €6,000 to €20,000 is common, then 20 to 40% of the price during construction, and the rest at the notary.

What taxes do I pay on a new build?

10% VAT on the price plus stamp duty (AJD) of 1.4% in the Valencian Community or 1.2% in Andalusia, plus notary, registry and legal fees.

What warranty do I get on a new home?

One year for finishing defects, three years for defects affecting habitability and ten years for the structure, under the Spanish Building Act.

Are new homes in Spain sold finished?

Yes. They come with a fitted kitchen, finished bathrooms and flooring, and on the coast usually air conditioning. Furniture is normally not included.

Can I get a mortgage on an off-plan home?

Yes. The mortgage is usually signed at completion, at the notary. Non-residents can typically borrow 60 to 70% of the price. See our guide to mortgages in Spain.
