Torrevieja – Lifestyle, Property Prices and Who Buys Here

By Lucyna Gacek · 6 January 2026 · updated 26 October 2026 · 3 min read

In short: Torrevieja is a year-round coastal city of more than 100,000 residents from over 100 countries, with some of the most affordable sea-side homes in Spain. Bank valuations averaged about €2,020 per m² in Q3 2026 (Tinsa), up 16% in a year, and asking prices are around €2,500 per m². Apartments start below €200,000. Buyers are Spanish, British, Scandinavian, German, Belgian and Dutch, and demand from both owners and tenants keeps homes easy to resell or rent.

Torrevieja grew from a salt-mining and fishing town into one of the most international cities in Spain. It is known for affordable prices, but it is much more than a bargain: a long seafront, two pink salt lagoons, a full range of services and a big community that lives here all year. Here is how the market looks in 2026.

Everyday life

Torrevieja is a real city, not a seasonal resort. With more than 100,000 registered residents, it is one of the largest municipalities in the province, so shops, health care, schools, sport and culture run all year.

Life is outdoors. People walk along the Juan Aparicio and Vista Alegre promenades, swim at Playa del Cura, Los Locos or La Mata, and cycle around the salt lagoons of La Mata and Torrevieja, a natural park where the water turns pink in summer. Costs are lower than in Alicante or on the Costa del Sol: a set lunch costs around €12–15 and a one-bedroom flat rents for roughly €650–900 a month.

Property prices in 2026

  • Valuations of completed homes (Tinsa, Q3 2026): about €2,020 per m², up 16.4% in a year.
  • Asking prices (idealista, mid-2026): around €2,500 per m² on average, from about €2,200 in the centre to around €3,000 near Playa de los Locos.

A resale one- or two-bedroom flat can still be found for €130,000–200,000; new apartments with a communal pool generally start around €220,000–250,000, and villas from about €350,000. Who buys in Torrevieja?

Residents come from more than 100 countries. Spanish buyers are the largest group, followed by the British, Swedes, Norwegians, Germans, Belgians and Dutch, with growing numbers from Central and Eastern Europe.

  • Retirees who want sunshine, flat walks and good hospitals.
  • Families and remote workers looking for affordable space near the sea.
  • Investors who rent long-term or to holidaymakers, as demand is high from both.

This mix makes Torrevieja liquid: homes sell and rent relatively easily.

Schools, health care and parks

  • Health care: the public Torrevieja University Hospital and the private Quirónsalud Torrevieja hospital.
  • Schools: public schools in town, and international schools nearby, including El Limonar International School in Villamartín.
  • Parks and nature: Parque de las Naciones, the Molino del Agua pine woods by La Mata beach and the lagoon natural park.

What is changing

The city is investing in its seafront and port, with more public space and a new promenade at Playa de los Locos. On the edge of town, the large La Hoya Lagoons Village project plans thousands of new energy-efficient homes around a swimmable lagoon, built in phases over many years.

Before buying, compare community fees, check the condition of older buildings and confirm the rules for holiday lets in the building and the area.

Frequently asked questions

How much does an apartment cost in Torrevieja?

Resale flats start at around €130,000–200,000. New apartments with a communal pool usually start at €220,000–250,000.

Is Torrevieja a good place to live all year?

Yes. It is a city of more than 100,000 people with a hospital, schools, shops and an active international community all year.

Have property prices in Torrevieja gone up?

Yes. Bank valuations rose about 16% in the year to Q3 2026, according to Tinsa.

Is Torrevieja good for rental investment?

Demand is strong both for long-term and holiday rentals. Returns depend on the building, community fees and the current tourist-rental rules.
