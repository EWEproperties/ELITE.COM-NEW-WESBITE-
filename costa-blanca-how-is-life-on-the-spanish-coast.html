Living in Spain

Costa Blanca: What Is Life Like on the Spanish Coast?

By Lucyna Gacek · 20 January 2025 · updated 26 October 2026 · 3 min read

In short: Life on the Costa Blanca means around 300 sunny days a year, winter highs of about 17 °C, a cost of living below most of northern Europe and one of the largest international communities in Spain. Alicante airport has flights to most European cities, and the AP-7 and TRAM connect the coast. The north (Jávea, Calpe, Altea) is greener and more expensive; the south (Torrevieja, Orihuela Costa) is flatter, drier and more affordable. Spain ranks 27th of 163 countries in the 2026 Global Peace Index.

The Costa Blanca stretches for more than 200 km along the province of Alicante, from Dénia to Pilar de la Horadada. For many people it starts as a holiday destination and becomes home, and Alicante province has one of the highest shares of foreign residents in Spain.

Here is an honest picture of daily life: what you gain, what it costs and which towns suit which kind of life.

Everyday life

Daily life here happens outdoors. Mornings start with a coffee on a terrace, many people walk or cycle along the seafront, and the weekly market is a social event. Winters are mild, with daytime highs around 17 °C in January, and summers are hot, around 30–31 °C in August. Rain is rare, but it can come in heavy autumn storms. Read more in the weather in Alicante.

Costs, healthcare and safety

  • Cost of living – food, eating out and transport are cheaper than in most of northern Europe. Housing prices have risen strongly since 2023, especially in the north and in Alicante city. See the cost of living in Spain.
  • Healthcare – large public hospitals in Alicante, Elche, Torrevieja, Dénia and Villajoyosa, plus many private clinics with international staff.
  • Safety – Spain is 27th of 163 countries in the 2026 Global Peace Index. On the coast the main risk is petty theft in busy places, so keep an eye on bags and phones. See is Spain safe?

Getting around

Alicante-Elche airport is about 10 km from Alicante city and has direct flights to most European cities all year. The AP-7 motorway and the N-332 run along the coast, and the TRAM links Alicante with Benidorm, Altea, Calpe and Dénia. Valencia airport is about 1 hour 30 minutes from the north of the coast, and Murcia airport is close to the south. A car is still useful for most residents outside the big towns.

The best towns to live in

  • Alicante – a real city with jobs, culture, universities and the airport next door.
  • Benidorm – services all year, great beaches and strong rental demand.
  • Calpe and Altea – sea and mountains, international but with a local feel.
  • Jávea and Dénia – green, low-rise and popular with families.
  • Torrevieja and Orihuela Costa – sunny, flat and the most affordable homes near the sea.
  • Cumbre del Sol – a quiet hillside resort with sea views, between Jávea and Moraira.

The international community

People from all over Europe live on the Costa Blanca, especially from the UK, the Netherlands, Belgium, Germany, Scandinavia and, more recently, Eastern Europe. There are international schools, clubs and associations in almost every town. At the same time, Spanish and Valencian traditions are strong, with local festivals such as the Hogueras in Alicante and Moors and Christians in many towns. Learning some Spanish makes a big difference.

Moving and buying

Most people rent for a few months first, then buy once they know the area. EU citizens who stay longer than three months register their residence and on the town hall's padrón. Read our step-by-step guide to moving to Spain and our guide to life after the move.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Costa Blanca a good place to live?

For many people, yes: a mild climate, lower living costs than northern Europe, good healthcare and an international community. The best town depends on your budget and lifestyle.

Which part of the Costa Blanca is best to live in?

The north (Jávea, Calpe, Altea) is greener and more upmarket. The south (Torrevieja, Orihuela Costa) is sunnier, flatter and more affordable. Alicante offers city life.

Is the Costa Blanca expensive?

Daily costs are moderate. Property prices vary widely, from some of the most affordable homes by the sea in the south to high prices in Jávea, Altea and Moraira.

How cold is the Costa Blanca in winter?

Mild. Daytime highs are around 17 °C in January, and nights are about 6–7 °C.
