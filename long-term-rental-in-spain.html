Long-Term Rental in Spain – What Landlords Need to Know in 2026

By Aleksandra Kobus · 16 January 2026 · updated 24 October 2026 · 4 min read

In short: A long-term let of a home in Spain falls under the Urban Leases Act (LAU). The tenant can stay up to 5 years (7 if the landlord is a company) even if the contract says one year, and can leave after 6 months with 30 days' notice. The deposit is one month's rent, plus at most two months in extra guarantees, and must be lodged with the regional authority. Yearly rent rises are capped by the INE's IRAV index (about 2.5% in 2026). The landlord pays the agency fees. Non-residents from the EU pay 19% tax on net rent; others pay 24% on gross rent.

Long-term rental is the quiet alternative to holiday lets: one tenant, steady monthly income, less wear and none of the tourist-licence rules. Demand is strong on the Costa Blanca and Costa del Sol, from local families, remote workers and people moving to Spain.

Spanish tenancy law protects tenants quite strongly, so it pays to know the rules before you sign. Here is how a long-term let works in 2026.

The contract: length and notice

Renting a home as the tenant's permanent residence is governed by the Urban Leases Act (Ley de Arrendamientos Urbanos, LAU). The key rules:

  • Minimum term. You can sign for one year, but the tenant can extend year by year up to 5 years, or 7 years if the landlord is a company.
  • Further extension. If neither side gives notice at the end, the contract extends by up to three more years, one at a time.
  • Tenant leaving early. After 6 months, with 30 days' notice. The contract can set a penalty of one month's rent for each remaining year.
  • Landlord needing the home back. Only if the contract says so, for the landlord or close family to live in, after the first year and with two months' notice.
  • Selling. The tenant has a right of first refusal unless the contract excludes it, and the lease continues with the new owner in most cases.

Contracts for a fixed season, such as a winter let or a stay for work or study (alquiler de temporada), are not covered by these protections. They must be genuine short stays, not a way around the law.

Deposit, rent increases and fees

  • Deposit (fianza): one month's rent, which must be lodged with the regional housing authority.
  • Extra guarantees: at most two more months' rent, as a deposit or bank guarantee, for leases up to 5 or 7 years. Many landlords also take rent-default insurance.
  • Rent increases: once a year, only if the contract says so. Since 2025 the increase is capped by the INE's rental update index (IRAV), around 2.5% in autumn 2026.
  • Agency fees: since 2023 the landlord pays the agency and the cost of drawing up the contract, not the tenant.

Some regions have "stressed areas" with rent caps. As of autumn 2026 there are none in the Valencian Community or Andalusia.

Preparing the home and finding a tenant

Before you rent out, you need:

  • an energy performance certificate, shown in the advert,
  • the occupancy licence or habitability certificate the region requires,
  • home insurance, and the utilities in working order (the contract says who pays them).

Tenants for good homes expect fast fibre internet, air conditioning and heating, a well-equipped kitchen and, for villas, a maintained pool and garden. Good photos and a clear description in Spanish and English help.

Check every candidate carefully: employment contract or proof of income, recent payslips or tax returns, ID and references. Ask for an inventory with photos signed by both sides at the start.

Managing from abroad

If you live outside Spain, a local property manager can find and check tenants, draw up the contract, lodge the deposit, collect the rent, handle repairs and file your non-resident tax returns. Fees are usually a percentage of the rent.

Long-term rental suits owners who want steady income and little involvement, and who do not need to use the home themselves. If you want to spend summers there, a seasonal winter let or holiday rental may fit better. Our guide to the holiday rental rules covers that option.

Frequently asked questions

How long can a tenant stay in Spain?

For a main home, up to 5 years (7 if the landlord is a company) even if the contract is for one year, plus up to 3 more years if nobody gives notice.

How much deposit can I ask for?

One month's rent as the legal deposit, plus at most two months' rent as an extra guarantee.

How much can I raise the rent each year?

Only once a year and only if the contract allows it. The rise is capped by the INE's IRAV index, about 2.5% in autumn 2026.

Who pays the agency fee for a long-term let?

The landlord. Since 2023 tenants no longer pay agency or contract fees for a home rented as their main residence.

Do I need a tourist licence for a long-term rental?

No. A tourist licence is only needed for holiday lets. A long-term let needs an energy certificate and the usual occupancy documents.
