Easter in Spain: Semana Santa Traditions

By Anita Orłowska · 8 August 2025 · updated 26 October 2026 · 3 min read

In short: Semana Santa (Holy Week) runs from Palm Sunday to Easter Sunday; in 2027 that is 21 to 28 March. Religious brotherhoods carry huge floats (pasos) through the streets, accompanied by hooded penitents and brass bands. The best known are in Seville and Málaga, while on the Costa Blanca Elche's Palm Sunday and Orihuela's processions are of International Tourist Interest. Good Friday is a national holiday, Holy Thursday is a holiday in most regions and Easter Monday in some, including the Valencian Community. Typical sweets are torrijas and, around Valencia, the mona de Pascua.

For a week each spring, Spanish towns change completely. Streets are closed for processions, balconies fill with spectators and the smell of incense and candle wax hangs in the air. Even people who are not religious take part, because Semana Santa is as much about community and family as about faith.

Here is what happens, where to see it and what it means for daily life if you live in Spain.

What happens during Holy Week

Each procession is organised by a cofradía or hermandad, a brotherhood that may be centuries old. Members carry or push heavy floats (pasos) with sculptures of Christ and the Virgin Mary, many of them artistic treasures. Penitents (nazarenos) walk in long robes and tall pointed hoods, some barefoot, and bands play solemn marches. In Andalusia you may hear a saeta, a sung prayer from a balcony as the float passes. Processions happen every day of the week, often late into the night.

The most famous celebrations

  • Seville – about 60 brotherhoods and the madrugá, the night of Holy Thursday to Good Friday, the emotional peak of the week.
  • Málaga – enormous thrones carried by up to 250 bearers, and on Holy Thursday the Spanish Legion carrying the Christ of the Good Death.
  • Valladolid and Zamora – silent, austere processions with some of Spain's finest religious sculpture.

Semana Santa on the Costa Blanca

You do not need to travel far. In Elche, Palm Sunday is celebrated with thousands of white palm fronds, braided by hand from the city's famous palm grove. Orihuela has one of the oldest and richest Holy Weeks in the region, with a museum dedicated to it. Both are declared of International Tourist Interest. Alicante, Torrevieja, Crevillent and many other towns also have their own processions.

Public holidays and daily life

Good Friday is a public holiday everywhere in Spain. Holy Thursday is a holiday in most regions, and Easter Monday in some, including the Valencian Community and Catalonia. Banks, offices and many shops close on those days, while restaurants and bars stay open and are busy. Schools have a week or more of holidays, and many Spaniards travel, so book hotels and car hire early. Traffic in town centres is often diverted for processions.

Easter food

  • Torrijas – bread soaked in milk or wine, fried and sweetened with honey or sugar and cinnamon.
  • Mona de Pascua – a sweet bread, sometimes with a boiled egg on top, eaten on Easter Monday in the Valencian Community, often on a picnic in the countryside.
  • Potaje de vigilia – a chickpea, spinach and salt cod stew, traditional on Good Friday.

Tips for visitors

  • Check the official procession timetables (itinerarios), published by each town.
  • Be respectful: do not cross through a procession, and keep quiet as the floats pass.
  • Expect crowds, especially in Seville and Málaga, and book accommodation months ahead.
  • Spring weather is usually pleasant, but evenings can be cool and rain can cancel processions.

Frequently asked questions

When is Semana Santa in 2027?

From Palm Sunday, 21 March, to Easter Sunday, 28 March 2027.

Is Easter a public holiday in Spain?

Good Friday is a national holiday. Holy Thursday is a holiday in most regions, and Easter Monday in some, such as the Valencian Community and Catalonia.

Where is the best Semana Santa in Spain?

Seville and Málaga are the most famous. On the Costa Blanca, Elche's Palm Sunday and Orihuela's processions are outstanding.

What do Spaniards eat at Easter?

Torrijas, a kind of sweet fried bread, are eaten everywhere. In the Valencian Community families share a mona de Pascua on Easter Monday.
