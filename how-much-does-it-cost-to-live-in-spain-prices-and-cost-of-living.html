Cost of Living in Spain 2026: What Everyday Life Costs

By Aleksandra Kobus · 25 January 2025 · updated 24 October 2026 · 4 min read

In short: A couple who own their home on the Costa Blanca or Costa del Sol typically spend around €1,800 to €2,800 a month on everyday life, including home running costs, food, a car, eating out and private health insurance. Renting a two-bedroom apartment adds roughly €900 to €1,600 a month, more in Marbella and Málaga. Fresh food, eating out and services are noticeably cheaper than in northern Europe, while electronics, cars and imported goods cost about the same. A menú del día lunch still costs around €12 to €18. For context, the Spanish minimum wage in 2026 is €1,221 a month paid 14 times a year.

Most of our buyers ask the same question once they have found a home: what will life here actually cost each month? The answer depends on where you live and how, but these are the figures we see on the coast in 2026.

A single person typically needs around 60 to 70% of this. Villas cost more to run: add a pool, a garden and higher electricity, often several hundred euros a month.

Running a home

  • Community fees pay for the shared areas, pool and building insurance. They are usually charged monthly or quarterly. See community of owners.
  • IBI, the yearly property tax, is typically a few hundred euros for an apartment on the coast. See IBI 2026.
  • Electricity is billed monthly. Bills are highest in July and August if you use air conditioning, and in winter if you heat with electricity, as most coastal homes have no central heating.
  • Water is billed monthly or every two or three months, often together with sewage and rubbish charges.
  • Fibre internet is fast and widely available, from around €25 to €35 a month, or less bundled with mobiles.

If you are away for long periods, budget for someone to look after the property and keep home insurance in place.

If you rent

Long-term rents have risen strongly. As a rough guide for a two-bedroom apartment on a yearly contract: €900 to €1,300 a month in Torrevieja, Orihuela Costa or Benidorm, €1,100 to €1,600 in Alicante city, Altea or Jávea, and €1,400 to €2,500 or more in Málaga, Estepona and Marbella. Expect to pay a deposit of one month plus up to two months of extra guarantee. See long-term rental in Spain.

Health care

  • Visitors from the EU use their European Health Insurance Card for necessary treatment in the public system during stays.
  • EU pensioners who move to Spain can usually register for public health care with the S1 form from their home country.
  • Other residents who are not covered can, after a year registered on the padrón, join the public system through the convenio especial: €60 a month under 65 and €157 a month from 65.
  • Private health insurance costs roughly €50 to €100 a month per person under 50 and considerably more after 65. It is required for some visas.
  • Private visits: a GP or specialist appointment is usually €60 to €120, a dental check-up and clean €40 to €80.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to live in Spain per month?

A couple who own their home on the coast typically spend €1,800 to €2,800 a month on everyday life. A single person needs about 60 to 70% of that. Renting adds roughly €900 to €1,600 a month for a two-bedroom apartment.

Is Spain cheaper than northern Europe?

For food, eating out, services and home running costs, generally yes. Cars, electronics and imported goods cost about the same.

How much are the monthly bills for an apartment?

Electricity, water, internet and community fees together usually come to €200 to €500 a month, depending on the building and your use of air conditioning.

Can foreigners use public health care in Spain?

EU visitors use the EHIC, EU pensioners can register with an S1 form, and other residents can join through the convenio especial after a year on the padrón, for €60 or €157 a month depending on age.

What is the minimum wage in Spain in 2026?

€1,221 a month paid in 14 instalments, or €17,094 a year.
