Buying Property in Spain Through a Company – When It Pays Off

By Anita Orłowska · 8 August 2025 · updated 24 October 2026 · 5 min read

In short: A company can buy Spanish property like a private person, but it only pays off in some cases: several rental properties, a real rental business, or property used by an existing company. The company pays 25% corporate tax on profit, can deduct costs and depreciate the building at up to 2% a year, but profits are taxed again when paid out to you. VAT on a new build is only recoverable if the company charges VAT, which plain holiday or long-term rentals do not. If owners use the home privately, it counts as taxable income. For one holiday home, buying in your own name is usually simpler and cheaper.

Most homes in Spain are bought by private people, but companies are active buyers too, especially investors with several properties and business owners who want to keep business and private assets apart.

Buying through a company can save tax in some cases and cost more in others. This guide explains when it makes sense, what taxes apply and how the purchase works.

Which company can buy?

There are three common routes:

  • A Spanish limited company (Sociedad Limitada, SL). It can be set up with as little as €1 of share capital, usually through a lawyer or gestor, and is taxed in Spain on all its income. This is the usual choice for a rental business in Spain.
  • An existing company in your home country. It needs a Spanish tax number (NIF) and its beneficial owners must be identified at the notary. Its Spanish rental income is taxed in Spain as non-resident income, and the company's home country has its own rules on top.
  • A Spanish branch of your existing company, for larger investors who want to operate in Spain without a separate company.

Avoid companies in tax havens. Spain charges a special yearly levy of 3% of the cadastral value on property owned by companies in non-cooperative jurisdictions.

The advantages

  • Costs are deductible: interest, community fees, IBI, insurance, repairs, management, cleaning and furniture.
  • Depreciation: the building (not the land) can be written off at up to 2% a year, furniture faster.
  • One tax rate: corporate tax is 25% on profit, instead of personal rates that can reach 47–54% for residents.
  • Separate assets: business risks stay in the company, and a portfolio is easier to manage, finance and pass on as shares.
  • Reinvesting: profits left in the company can fund the next purchase without personal income tax.

The disadvantages

  • Double taxation when you take money out. Profits pay 25% in the company and then 19–30% as dividends in Spain, or tax at home.
  • Running costs. Bookkeeping, yearly accounts, corporate tax returns and VAT returns cost money every year, even when the property earns little.
  • Private use is taxed. If you or your family stay in the home for free, the market rent counts as income for you, and the company may lose the right to deduct costs for those periods.
  • Small-company rates do not apply. A company that only holds property is usually treated as an asset-holding company (entidad patrimonial) and pays the full 25%.
  • Harder mortgages. Banks lend to companies, but ask for more guarantees, often personal ones from the owners.
  • Wealth tax still applies. Shares in a company whose assets are mainly Spanish property are taxed in Spain, also for non-residents.

VAT on new builds

Renting a home long term is exempt from VAT, and so is a holiday rental without hotel-type services. In those cases the company cannot recover the 10% VAT paid on a new build. Only a tourist rental that offers hotel services such as daily cleaning, linen changes and reception charges VAT and can recover it. This is the most common misunderstanding about company purchases.

When does a company make sense?

A company is usually worth it if:

  • you plan to buy several properties to rent out and reinvest the income,
  • you run a real rental or hotel-type business with staff and services,
  • the property is used by an existing business, for example as offices or staff housing,
  • you want to pass a portfolio on to the next generation as shares.

For one holiday home that you use yourself and rent out now and then, buying in your own name is almost always simpler and cheaper. Ask a tax adviser to compare both routes for your situation and your home country before you sign.

Buying through a company, step by step

  1. Get tax advice in both countries and choose the structure.
  2. Set up or register the company: incorporate an SL, or get a Spanish NIF for your foreign company with its registry extract, articles and the director's powers, apostilled and translated.
  3. Open a Spanish company bank account.
  4. Check the property as any buyer would: Nota Simple, debts, licences and, for rentals, the tourist licence.
  5. Sign the deposit contract and the deed at the notary. The director signs, or someone with a company power of attorney.
  6. Pay the taxes and register the property in the company's name.
  7. Keep the books, file quarterly and yearly returns, and invoice any private use.

The rest of the process is the same as for a private buyer. Our guide to buying property in Spain explains each step.

Frequently asked questions

Can a foreign company buy property in Spain?

Yes. It needs a Spanish tax number (NIF), its documents apostilled and translated, and its beneficial owners identified at the notary. A Spanish SL is often simpler for a rental business in Spain.

Can a company recover the VAT on a new apartment?

Only if its activity is subject to VAT, such as a tourist rental with hotel-type services. Long-term rentals and plain holiday rentals are VAT-exempt, so the 10% VAT is a cost.

Can I use a company-owned holiday home myself?

Yes, but private use is taxed. The market rent for your stay counts as income for you, and the company may lose deductions for that period.

Is buying through a company cheaper for one holiday home?

Usually not. Set-up costs, yearly accounts and tax on dividends normally outweigh the savings. A company pays off mainly for several rentals or a real business.

What corporate tax does a Spanish property company pay?

Generally 25% on profit. Companies that only hold property usually cannot use the reduced rates for small companies.
