The Best Places to Live in Spain for Retirees

By Anita Orłowska · 19 September 2025 · updated 26 October 2026 · 4 min read

In short: For retirees the best places combine a flat, walkable centre, a hospital within about 20–30 minutes, an airport within an hour and an active community. On the Costa Blanca that means Alicante, Torrevieja, Benidorm and Altea or, for a greener and quieter life, Jávea and Dénia. On the Costa del Sol, Fuengirola, Benalmádena, Marbella and Estepona. Los Alcázares and San Pedro on the Mar Menor are the most affordable.

Sunshine, a slower pace and good food make Spain one of Europe's favourite places to retire. But the best town for a 40-year-old buyer is not always the best for a 70-year-old. Hills, distances to the hospital and how busy a place is in winter matter more with every year. This guide compares the main areas we know well, so you can choose a place that will still suit you in twenty years.

What makes a good place to retire?

  • Flat and walkable: shops, a pharmacy, cafés and the health centre within walking distance, without steep hills.
  • Health care nearby: a public hospital within 20–30 minutes and private clinics close by.
  • Good connections: an airport within an hour for family visits, and buses or a train if you stop driving.
  • Life all year: a town that stays open and sociable in winter, with clubs and other residents of your age.
  • The right home: a lift, few steps, a ground floor or a single-level villa, and a sunny terrace.

Costa Blanca

The Costa Blanca has the widest choice and lower prices than the Costa del Sol. Alicante airport serves both the north and south of the coast. Torrevieja and Orihuela Costa are popular for their flat promenades, many services in English and other languages, and apartments from well under €200,000. Benidorm, Altea and L'Alfàs del Pi have a large year-round community of northern European retirees and lots of clubs and activities. Jávea, Moraira and Dénia in the north are greener and quieter, with more villas, but also more hills, so choose a home with level access.

Costa del Sol

The Costa del Sol is more international and upmarket, with Málaga airport close to every town. Fuengirola and Benalmádena are flat and compact, with a train line to Málaga and the airport, which is a big plus if you no longer want to drive. Marbella and Estepona offer golf, beautiful old towns and a high level of private health care, at higher prices. For more on this coast, see our comparison Costa Blanca or Costa del Sol.

Mar Menor (Murcia)

Towns such as Los Alcázares and San Pedro del Pinatar sit on a calm salt-water lagoon with shallow, warm water and long flat promenades. Homes are among the cheapest on the Spanish coast, and the region has its own airport (Región de Murcia, Corvera), with Alicante about an hour away. It is quieter in winter than the bigger towns.

Health care for retirees

Spain's public health system is good, and much of the coast has modern hospitals. Pensioners from the EU, the EEA, Switzerland and the UK who receive a state pension from home usually get public cover by registering an S1 form with the Spanish social security. Others need private health insurance. For older applicants it often costs €100–€300 or more a month, and some insurers do not accept new customers over a certain age, so arrange it early. Many retirees keep private cover alongside public care for faster access to specialists and English-speaking doctors.

Residency and practical steps

  • EU, EEA and Swiss citizens: register as resident (certificado de registro) with proof of income and health cover, and sign on the town hall's padrón.
  • UK and other non-EU citizens: usually apply for a non-lucrative residence visa, which needs a fixed minimum income (about €2,400 a month for one person) and private health insurance.
  • Tax: if you live in Spain more than 183 days a year, you become tax resident. Pensions are taxed according to the tax treaty with your home country. Our guide resident vs non-resident explains the difference, and retirement life in Spain covers budgets and daily life.

Frequently asked questions

Where is the best place to retire in Spain?

It depends on your budget and lifestyle. For flat, lively towns with good hospitals, Alicante, Torrevieja, Benidorm, Fuengirola and Marbella are popular. For a quieter, greener life, Jávea and Dénia. For the lowest prices, the Mar Menor.

Can I use Spanish public health care as a retiree?

Yes, if you receive a state pension from an EU country, the EEA, Switzerland or the UK and register your S1 form. Otherwise you need private health insurance.

How much do I need to retire in Spain?

Many couples live comfortably on €1,800–€2,500 a month after housing costs on the coast. Non-EU citizens need about €2,400 a month for one person to qualify for a non-lucrative visa.

Which coast is cheaper for retirees?

The Costa Blanca and the Mar Menor are clearly cheaper than the Costa del Sol, both for buying and for everyday life.
