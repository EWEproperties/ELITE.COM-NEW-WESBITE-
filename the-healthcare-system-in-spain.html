The Healthcare System in Spain

By Aleksandra Kobus · 4 July 2025 · updated 26 October 2026 · 3 min read

In short: Spain's public health system is free at the point of use for residents who work, receive a pension (including EU and UK pensioners with an S1 form) or are family members of someone covered. Others can join the public pay-in scheme after a year of residence, or take private insurance. Emergency care is available to everyone. Private policies start at around €40–60 a month for young adults and cost more with age.

For anyone moving to Spain or spending long periods here, health care is one of the first things to arrange. The good news is that Spain has one of the best health systems in Europe, with modern hospitals along the whole coast. Here is who can use it, how to register and when private insurance makes sense.

How the system is organised

The public system (Sistema Nacional de Salud) is funded by taxes and run by each region. In the Valencian Community it is the Conselleria de Sanitat, in Andalusia the Servicio Andaluz de Salud (SAS), in Murcia the Servicio Murciano de Salud. Your first contact is the local health centre (centro de salud), where you are assigned a family doctor (médico de cabecera). Specialists and hospital care are by referral from that doctor, except in an emergency.

Who can use public health care?

  • Workers and the self-employed paying Spanish social security, and their registered family members.
  • Pensioners with a Spanish pension, or with a state pension from another EU country, the EEA, Switzerland or the UK, who register their S1 form.
  • Residents without any other cover who have been registered on the padrón for at least a year can join the convenio especial, a public pay-in scheme. In 2026 it costs about €60 a month under 65 and about €157 a month from 65.
  • Visitors from the EU use their European Health Insurance Card (EHIC); UK visitors use the GHIC. These cover necessary care during a temporary stay, not a move.

Emergency care in a public hospital is always provided, whatever your status. Call 112 for an ambulance.

How to register and get your health card

  1. Get your NIE and, if you move here, your residence certificate or TIE.
  2. Register on the padrón at your town hall.
  3. Get a social security number, or register your S1 form with the social security (INSS).
  4. Take these documents to your local health centre and ask to be registered.

You then receive a health card: the tarjeta SIP in the Valencian Community or the tarjeta sanitaria in Andalusia and Murcia. Show it at every appointment and at the pharmacy.

What you pay in the public system

Doctor visits, tests, hospital stays and operations are free. Prescription medicines have a co-payment that depends on income: most working people pay 40–60% of the price, pensioners usually 10% with a low monthly cap, and people on the lowest incomes nothing. Adult dental care, glasses and most physiotherapy are not covered, so most people pay for them privately.

Private health insurance

Many foreign residents use both systems: public care for emergencies and serious treatment, private care for faster appointments with specialists and doctors who speak English. The main insurers include Sanitas, Adeslas, DKV, Asisa, Mapfre, Caser and AXA.

  • Young adults: from about €40–60 a month for a basic policy.
  • Ages 50–65: often €80–150 a month.
  • Over 65: €150–300 or more, and some insurers do not accept new customers above a certain age.

For a non-lucrative or digital nomad visa you need a policy with full cover in Spain and no co-payments or waiting periods. Check exclusions for existing conditions before you sign.

Practical tips for foreigners

  • Language: in tourist areas many doctors speak English, and some public hospitals on the coast have interpreters, but outside big towns Spanish helps a lot.
  • Pharmacies (farmacia, green cross) give good advice for minor problems, and one in each area is always open at night (farmacia de guardia).
  • Location: when choosing a home, check the distance to the nearest public hospital and private clinics. Our guide to the best places for retirees lists the hospitals near each town.

Frequently asked questions

Is healthcare free in Spain for foreigners?

It is free at the point of use for residents who are covered: workers, pensioners with an S1 form or a Spanish pension, and their families. Others can join the public pay-in scheme or take private insurance. Emergency care is always provided.

How much is private health insurance in Spain?

From about €40–60 a month for young adults, €80–150 for people aged 50–65 and €150–300 or more over 65, depending on the insurer and cover.

Can UK pensioners use Spanish public health care?

Yes. UK state pensioners living in Spain can register an S1 form, issued by the UK, and get public care on the same terms as Spanish pensioners.

What is the convenio especial?

A public pay-in scheme for residents with no other cover, available after a year on the padrón. In 2026 it costs about €60 a month under 65 and €157 from 65.

Plan your health cover before you move

Sorting out health cover early makes the move far less stressful. Check which route applies to you, register as soon as you have your padrón, and choose a home within easy reach of good care. If you would like help finding a home close to hospitals and services, contact us.
