Staying in Spain for More Than 90 Days While Owning Property

By Anita Orłowska · 5 May 2025 · updated 24 October 2026 · 4 min read

In short: Owning a home in Spain does not give you the right to live there. EU, EEA and Swiss citizens can stay as long as they like but must register as residents after three months. British and other non-EU owners can stay only 90 days in any 180 without a visa; to stay longer, most use the non-lucrative visa. Once you spend more than 183 days a year in Spain, you usually become tax resident.

Many owners would like to spend the whole winter, or more, in their Spanish home. What you need to do depends above all on your passport. Here is how it works for EU citizens and for British and other non-EU owners.

EU, EEA and Swiss citizens

As an EU, EEA or Swiss citizen you can enter Spain with your passport or national ID card and stay up to three months without any formalities.

If you stay longer, or move to Spain, you must register within three months of arriving and get a registration certificate (certificado de registro de ciudadano de la UE), often called the green certificate. You apply at the foreigners' office or a national police station with an appointment, and you show:

  • your passport or ID card,
  • proof of income or savings, such as a pension or bank statements,
  • health cover: public (for example an S1 form) or private insurance without co-payments.

The certificate also includes your NIE number. After five years of legal residence you can apply for permanent residence.

British and other non-EU citizens

Since Brexit, British citizens follow the same rules as other non-EU visitors. You can stay up to 90 days in any 180-day period across the whole Schengen area without a visa. Owning property in Spain does not change this, and the golden visa for property buyers ended in April 2025.

Since the EU's Entry/Exit System started in October 2025, your entries and exits are recorded digitally at the border, so overstays are easy to spot and can lead to fines and entry bans.

To spend more than 90 days in Spain, most retired or financially independent owners apply for the non-lucrative residence visa at the Spanish consulate before travelling. In 2026 you need about €28,800 a year in income or savings for one person, plus about €7,200 for each family member, and private health insurance. Remote workers can use the digital nomad visa instead.

Other registrations

  • NIE. Every foreign owner needs this tax identification number to buy, pay taxes, open a bank account and sign up for utilities. It is not a residence permit.
  • Padrón. Register at your town hall (empadronamiento) as soon as you live there. You need it for healthcare, school places, some tax reductions and, in many towns, discounts on local services.
  • Digital certificate. Once you have your NIE, getting a digital certificate makes dealing with the tax office and other authorities online much easier.

When do you become tax resident?

Residence for immigration and residence for tax are two different things. You are usually tax resident in Spain if you spend more than 183 days here in a calendar year (the days do not have to be consecutive), or if your main business or family is here.

  • As a tax resident, you file a Spanish income tax return (IRPF) on your worldwide income and declare foreign assets over €50,000 per category.
  • As a non-resident, you pay non-resident income tax (IRNR) on your Spanish home every year, even if you do not rent it out.
  • Every owner pays IBI, the local property tax.

Tax treaties between Spain and most countries prevent you paying tax twice on the same income. Get advice before you pass 183 days for the first time.

Healthcare

The European Health Insurance Card (EHIC), or the UK's GHIC, covers necessary treatment during temporary stays only. It is not enough to register as a resident.

If you live in Spain and receive a state pension from another EU country or the UK, you can usually register an S1 form with the Spanish social security office and then get a public health card (tarjeta sanitaria) from your local health centre. If you work in Spain, you are covered through your social security contributions. Otherwise, you need private insurance without co-payments.

Driving and cars

  • EU driving licences stay valid in Spain. If you live here, you renew it in Spain when it expires.
  • UK licences can be used by visitors. Residents must exchange theirs for a Spanish licence within six months of becoming resident.
  • Foreign-registered cars can be used in Spain for up to six months a year by non-residents. Once you are resident, you must register the car in Spain, which involves a technical inspection and a registration tax.

Frequently asked questions

Does owning a property in Spain give me residency?

No. Owning a home gives you no right to live in Spain. EU citizens can live here anyway; non-EU citizens, including the British, need a visa to stay more than 90 days in 180.

How long can British homeowners stay in Spain?

Up to 90 days in any 180-day period without a visa. For longer stays, most use the non-lucrative residence visa.

Do EU citizens need to register in Spain?

Yes, if they stay more than three months. They get a registration certificate from the foreigners' office or police by showing ID, income and health cover.

When do I become tax resident in Spain?

Usually when you spend more than 183 days a year in Spain, or when your main business or family is here.
